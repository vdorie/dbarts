## Steps

1. setdata-names-refused (dec-B478). setData and setPredictor refuse names that are not the sampler's: a frame, or a matrix that carries column names, whose names are not the sampler's is refused, naming the sampler's names that are missing; the sampler's own names in another order are matched by name as setData did; a matrix with no column names is read by position with no message, as is any data handed to a sampler that has no names. Test: test-setdata-factor-levels.R and test-sampler-setData.R restated (y ~ x.new on a y ~ x sampler), and the new pins in test-setdata-records.R or a file of their own: setData and setPredictor over frames, named and unnamed matrices, a permutation, a subset, a superset, and the sampler left as it was. Consumers checked: stan4bart, bartCause, treatSens. NEWS restates the setData sentence.
2. updatescale-no-scale-families (dec-B481). The refusal of updateScale = TRUE on a gp sampler holding saved draws (src/R_interface_bartcore.cpp refuseSavedGPDrawReanchor, holdsSavedGPDraws) skips probit, logistic and ordinal, which have no response scale, and stays on gaussian, Student-t, aft and nbinom; the flat C API's setResponse and setOffset entries follow, with no signature changed. Test: probit, logistic and ordinal for setResponse, setOffset, setWeights, setActiveRows and setData taking the call, and the refusal kept on gaussian, in test-setdata-records.R and its neighbors; tests/cpp plain and under ASAN/UBSAN.
3. predict-training-na-action (dec-B482). predict with no newdata follows na.action for a missing weight or offset exactly as newdata does, by the one resolver (R/data.R resolvePredictRows) the newdata path runs: the default refuses with the same hint, na.pass gives those rows NA draws, na.omit drops them, na.exclude and na.fail as at newdata. The type-by-type reading of weights is as it was. bartBT's help drops the stated difference. Test: test-predict-training-rows.R, every type and family, against the call at newdata = the training data.
4. training-bases-null-hint (dec-B483). The refusal of a NULL entry for a forest that carries a basis, with no newdata, names fit$bases and the idiom b <- fit$bases; b[[2]] <- z1, with the forest's index. Test: test-predict-blend.R.
5. trees-forest-labels (dec-B484). The forest column of extract(fit, "trees") on a fit is a factor whose levels are the forests' labels in forest order, forest<i> where a forest has none; the sampler's $getTrees stays numbered. Every route that returns the fit's tree table is checked, and the tests that compare the column with integers are restated. Test: test-forest-labels.R and the restated files.

## Calls made in the build

None was put to the maintainer. Each is the narrowest call that the ruling's words and its neighbors allowed, with the alternative.

- Item 1, a matrix that carries column names is held to the rule as a frame is, at setPredictor as at setData (where it is data with names anyway): names that are not the sampler's are refused, and the sampler's own in another order are matched by name, as predict and setTestPredictor match a named matrix. The alternative is a named matrix read by position at setPredictor, refusing only frames. It left a silent misread of a permuted named matrix, and gave setData and setPredictor two rules for one input. test-sampler-predictors.R's dimnames test handed a named matrix of other names to a named sampler to show the incoming names win; it now hands it to a sampler with no names, where they do.
- Item 1, a sampler with no column names takes data that carry names by position with no message, and its record takes the data's names, as before; the alternative is to refuse. It has no names for the data's to differ from. Unnamed data and a sampler with names read by position and say nothing, as ruled.
- Item 1, the refusal runs before setData's sparse early return, so a named sparse or mixed source with other names is refused by name; the bridge still refuses a sparse source for its own reason, and a sparse source whose names are the sampler's in another order is not permuted, which setData does not do for sparse sources at all. The alternative is to leave sparse sources unchecked.
- Item 1, a sampler whose own names repeat takes the same names in the same places, and any other order of the same names is refused ("cannot be matched by name, a name repeating"), since a repeated name names two places; a rename is refused by the foreign-name text. TODO setdata-repeated-names (x, b, x, d handed as x, b, y, d, refused with "the data's column 'x' is in the place of the sampler's 'x'") is a rename, which dec-B478 refuses by name, so its confusing text is gone; the permutation of repeated names stays refused.
- Item 1, the consumers: stan4bart (bartcore), bartCause (dbarts-1.0) and treatSens (master and dbarts-1.0) never call setData; treatSens calls setTestPredictor, which is unchanged; bairrtt's setPredictor calls are the column form. Nothing needed a change.
- Item 2, the family test is made on the sampler's engine family: gaussian (Student-t and the heteroscedastic variants are gaussian in the engine), aft and nbinom keep the refusal; probit, logistic and ordinal skip it. The standardization refusal (`updateStandardization`) and setData's refusal of a call that derives or takes another standardization stay for every family, so a probit gp sampler holding saved draws still refuses `setWeights(updateStandardization = TRUE)` and a fresh data object at setData. A multinomial sampler is refused by the multi-forest guard before the gp rule. An aft sampler's setWeights is refused by the gp rule first, ahead of its own "no case weights".
- Item 2, the header: dbarts.h's text for setResponse said that updateScale true "is also refused, whatever y holds, on a sampler with gp leaves that holds saved draws", and setOffset's "as does a sampler with gp leaves that holds saved draws, whatever the offset"; both stated the old refusal and now carry the family. Only comments changed: no declaration, enumerator or struct, so DBARTS_C_API_HASH is the same, which the compile-time static_assert of C_interface.cpp confirmed on every build and test-capi's `matches.header` on the installed one. docs/design/leaf-conversions.md's paragraph on the refusal said it read neither the values nor the family and listed a probit response among the refused; it says what the rule now is.
- Item 3, a missing weight or offset marks its row incomplete, the row is computed at a stand-in (weight 1, offset 0), and the result has the row set to NA or cut. This leaves the random stream and every other row's draw as a call stating the stand-ins draws, the way a weight of 0 is already drawn at 1 and set to NA. The posterior predictive draws of the kept rows under na.pass or na.omit are therefore not those of the call at newdata with the rows removed, whose stream is shorter; ev, bart, forest, class and the band are equal to it to rounding. The alternative is to subset the stored draws by row before drawing, which needs a per-class row subset of every stored channel (yhat.train, latent.train, s.train, sigma pairings) for one stream equality.
- Item 3, na.action is looked at only when a per-row offset or weights has a missing value, as predict.lm with no newdata does not look at it: a call with complete channels accepts any na.action it accepted before, a name that is no function included. At newdata na.action is resolved on every call.
- Item 3, a missing weight marks its row on every type, ev, bart, forest and class included, as at newdata, though the type reads no weight; a lone NA is an error under every action. The form of the offset and of the weights is as it was and no longer says "none missing": survivalProbabilities keeps its own "none missing", which the ruling does not name and test-aft.R pins. hurdle has no offset or weights channel and does not consult na.action.
- Item 3, padPredictedRows takes a `full` record for a result computed on every row, so the one routine that pads and cuts newdata's rows does the training rows' too; resolvePredictRows, predictChannelIncomplete and keptRowsRecord are the newdata path's, unchanged.
- Item 4, the idiom in the message names the first entry the caller gave, else the forest refused: `list(NULL, z1, NULL)` names `b[[2]]`, and `list(NULL, NULL)` on a two-forest fit names `b[[2]]`. The ruling's `b[[2]] <- z1` is the forest the caller changes and the message cannot know which of several was meant; the alternative is the forest refused, which says to replace the one the caller left NULL. The refusal at newdata keeps its words.
- Item 5, a fit of one forest declared with `forests = list(only = forest())` has the level "forest1", not "only": a model of one records no labels (attr(control, "bartcore.forests") is not kept for it), so it has none, in dec-B475's words. Recording one is a change to how a one-forest declaration is created and is not named. The sampler's own $getTrees, the sampler's other readers and rbart's table (which has no forest column) are unchanged; no package code, stan4bart, bartCause or treatSens reads the column, and the vignette and bartBT's Extracting Trees section say what it is.
