# Referee report: how k-move-pricing chooses its held-out real datasets

2026-11-07. Read: the plan on k-move-pricing at 59a3dc165 (Goal, Common to steps 1 to 3 with "Seen and held
out", step 2 reading 2, Step 3, Sizing and stops, Calls), survey.md, audit.md, the four papers the survey cited
secondhand, the seven papers for the audit's unknowns, Chawla et al. 2002, and as checks Boulesteix, Wilson and
Hapfelmeier 2017, Erickson et al. 2025, Chipman et al. 2010, Demsar 2006, Polson et al. 2013, Chopin and
Ridgway 2017, and Corani et al. 2017 (arXiv v3, fetched to scratch/papers/). Documentation only: UCI and OpenML
records and the TabArena curation sheet. No data file was downloaded and no outcome column was read.

## Verdict: ACCEPT WITH CHANGES (major)

The survey's rule B is the right shape: a frame curated by others for another purpose (TabArena), full
enrollment, a fixed screen with a flow table, and a sealed stratified split with the seed fixed before any
score. Boulesteix et al. (2017) ask for this, and the curated suites do it. A referee would take it over the
plan's hand-made list of 26, and rule B also repairs a real defect in the plan: as written, the plan leaves the
top-scoring blocks as reserves.

The pool the rule yields cannot be accepted as it stands. The audit passes 12 datasets against the 18 the two
uses need. My checks remove 3 more for certain and up to 6 under rules the audit applies elsewhere, leaving
6 to 9. The only tier-1 passers that survive every consistency fix are online shoppers, credit-card default and
qsar-biodeg, against the 6 that R2 needs if R2 is drawn from tier 1. The rule has to be widened by a fixed
published order before anything is scored. The R2 guard's precision also rests on an unsourced standard error.

## Required changes

1. **Widen the frame by a fixed, published order, settle the doubts a file can settle, and write both rules
   down before any score is computed.**
   - Why: under doubt-fails, 6 to 9 datasets pass, against 18 plus reserves; the audit's own count is 12 and
     5 in tier 1.
   - Order: TabArena-v0.1 binary; then OpenML-CC18's unseen binary tasks, the most cited published suite
     (Bischl et al. 2021); then TabArena's tier 2; then a seeded UCI census (the survey's rule C) only if still
     short.
   - Doubts a covariate-only check can settle (duplicate rows, repeated id columns, row counts; audit
     section 5) are settled by that check before the split. A doubt no file can settle fails.
   - If the pool is still short, the guard and the test run at the counts that pass and the report says so. No
     dataset is added by hand.
   - Sources: Boulesteix et al. 2017 (criteria fixed beforehand, no post-hoc tuning of inclusion, exclusions in
     a flowchart); the audit's sections 4 and 5.

2. **Correct the audit's passes, and have a second auditor screen independently before the list is
   committed.** The survey's own screen asks for two auditors, and the audit was one. My sample found errors
   in 5 of its 12 passes:
   - **irish fails on leakage.** OpenML record 451 carries the tag "label_leakage". Its covariates include
     "Educational level attained" and a school type "primary terminal leaver", both recorded after the
     Leaving Certificate outcome. The audit marked its leak column P.
   - **CKD is U, not P,** by the audit's own row: its Dup/leak column is U. UCI gives no hospital, no intro
     paper and no class criteria.
   - **Rice and raisin share a source.** Their UCI creators are Cinar and Koklu (raisin adds Tasdemir), and
     both come from the same machine-vision pipeline. The survey's criterion 4 allows one dataset per source,
     so one of the two goes.
   - **Coimbra and sa-heart must be judged by one case-control rule.** The audit fails sa-heart on its outcome
     column for being case-control. It passes Coimbra, whose UCI labels are "1=Healthy controls 2=Patients"
     (Patricio et al. 2018 recruited 64 patients and 52 controls). Fail both on that ground or neither.
     sa-heart fails anyway on its post-treatment measurements.
   - **GiveMeSomeCredit, Bioresponse and heloc must be judged by one license rule.** The audit passes the
     first two with the Kaggle terms unread and marks heloc U for unread terms.

3. **Apply the papers to the unknowns.** None of the papers moves a dataset to P.
   - **oil_spill: F.** Kubat et al. (1998) have 937 regions from 9 images and write that "examples are
     naturally grouped in batches... drawn from the same image".
   - **mammography: F.** Chawla et al. (2002) describe "classification of pixels in mammogram images": 11,183
     samples, 260 calcifications. Rows nest within images whatever the count of patients.
   - **jm1: F.** Gray et al. (2012, figure 2) show about 18 percent repeated data points, above the 10 percent
     bar; its defect flag is "error count >= 1". Shepperd et al. (2013) confirm that every NASA set has
     duplicate cases.
   - **Taiwanese bankruptcy: F by doubt.** Liang et al. (2016) describe 239 bankrupt and 239 matched
     non-bankrupt firms, each variable min-max normalized. The file's 6,819 rows and 220 positives are not
     described anywhere, so the unit of a row is undocumented.
   - **Polish bankruptcy: stays F by doubt.** Rows are financial statements, and class tracks the observation
     window. Zieba et al. (2016) observe bankrupt firms in 2007 to 2013 and operating firms in 2000 to 2012.
     The UCI page, and the audit after it, have the two windows reversed.
   - **hazelnut: F by doubt.** Ricci et al. (2021) describe 1,240 samples (600 clean, 640 contaminated), not
     the 2,400-row balanced file. The contamination was recreated in jars, with oil in place of the spread, and
     test accuracy reached 99.35 percent. The file's likely source, Urbinati et al. 2020, was not read.
   - **SPECT and SPECTF.** Kurgan et al. (2001) give one row per patient, so independence is P. The outcome
     "normal" versus "abnormal" groups seven overall diagnoses; that is a merge under the audit's rule, which
     also failed vertebral column. See change 4.
   - **Mammographic mass: stays U.** Elter et al. (2007) have 961 "mass regions" and say nothing on masses
     per patient.

4. **State the outcome rule so it can be applied the same way everywhere.** Recommended: "the binary task as
   the originating study or donor defined it; no merge or threshold made by a later curator or by this study".
   - Why: as written, the screen fails vertebral column's donor two-class file. It passes thoracic surgery's
     "death within one year", which is equally a threshold.
   - Under this rule, SPECT, vertebral (two classes), Debrecen and jm1 pass on outcome; they may still fail on
     other grounds. Diabetes130US, myocardial infarction and red wine fail.
   - The suites' exclusions are aimed at curator-made binarizations (Bischl et al. 2021; Gijsbers et al.
     2024).
   - A source with several candidate targets and no default (cervical cancer, colic) fails, because choosing
     its target would be our call.
   - The alternative is the literal no-threshold rule, applied everywhere, which also fails thoracic and
     credit default.

5. **Allocation.**
   - Keep the survey's repair: blocks span the whole score range, and the seed is fixed before scoring.
   - Drop "R2 from tier 1 only". The survey itself says high-separation data come mainly from tier 2, so this
     clause keeps out of the guard the data where the move acts. Use a row floor instead, fixed before
     scoring (for example at least 1,000 rows, so the test fold holds at least 200).
   - A reserve replaces only a dataset that fails the pre-fit file check, never one that fails after a fit.
   - State now whether real datasets scoring 0.995 or more stay in. I recommend in, reported apart: users fit
     such data, and the regimes' deterministic rejection was written for simulated truths.

6. **R2's standard error and its reading.**
   - The plan's "log score se about 0.002 a dataset" has no derivation in the plan.
   - Nadeau and Bengio (2003, section 4, item 6) correct the variance of the mean over J splits to
     (1/J + n2/n1) S^2. At J = 20 and 80/20 that is 6 times the naive S^2/J, so the standard error is 2.45
     times the naive one. The survey has this right.
   - The naive S^2/J is unbiased only for the variance conditional on the dataset (their section 4, item 2).
     It supports a claim about the average over all splits of these rows, not about the sampler's error at
     that training size.
   - State the target, and use the corrected form per dataset; Benavoli et al. and Corani et al. (2017) call
     it the correlated t-test.
   - For a difference between two samplers of one posterior, much of which is Monte Carlo noise, the
     correction errs conservative (their ratio falls below 1 when the true correlation is below
     n2/(n1+n2)). It can leave a dataset unresolved but never falsely harmless.
   - At a corrected standard error of 0.005, one dataset resolves at a true 0 about half the time and all six
     about 2 percent of the time. Resolving with probability 0.9 needs 0.0031 or less, which more splits barely
     buy.
   - State the split design under the 4,000-row training cap. If test rows come from the remainder, n2/n1 is
     not the overlap the correction assumes, and the design must say how the variance is then taken.
   - Say whether the guard holds per dataset (each of the six passes) or pooled. Corani et al. (2017) give the
     published model for both: a hierarchical model over datasets with Nadeau and Bengio's correlation and a
     region of practical equivalence.

7. **Name what has no published model (dec-B633):**
   - the 0.01-nat margin (the September study's own);
   - the separation score and its bands;
   - step 3's "needs the move" (the k study's acceptance, the 0.02 bar, a t bound on 20 fits);
   - the doubt-fails screen, whose nearest published model is TabArena's manual curation.

   Step 3 is a diagnostic-accuracy design; STARD 2015 is its reporting model (Bossuyt et al., BMJ 351:h5527,
   doi 10.1136/bmj.h5527; found by search, not read). The survey's "as diagnostic-accuracy studies require a
   spread of cases" carries no citation.

8. **Write step 3's expected reading before any fit, as step 2 has one.**
   - Give the chance of at least 3 resolved datasets on each side, given that 10 of the k study's 12
     high-score references failed acceptance.
   - Say plainly that the audited pool has nothing under a 0.06 outcome rate, and few near-separated
     candidates once the reputational guesses (CKD, vote) are set aside.

## Optional changes

- Report the move on the sampler literature's standard binary sets, labeled as seen and outside any guard. They
  are Polson et al. 2013 (nodal, diabetes, heart, Australian, German) and Chopin and Ridgway 2017 (adding
  breast, musk, sonar, DNA). This allows comparison with that literature. Nearly all are seen or share a source
  with a seen dataset, so they cannot be held out.
- Report the Brier score beside the log score; it is the other strictly proper score for binary outcomes.
- Report a pooled across-dataset reading beside the per-dataset one: the sign test or signed rank of Demsar
  (2006), whose best one-sided p at six datasets is 1/64.
- Correct the survey's small errors:
  - the plan strikes 7 datasets, not 6;
  - the 20 splits are not shown to come from Chipman et al. (2010), who used 20 random splits at 5/6 to 1/6,
    not 80/20.

## Questions put to the referee

- **Fixed before data, no room to steer?** Yes in form, once changes 1, 4 and 5 are made.
  - The score is sealed, the seed precedes it, and the screen only removes datasets.
  - The steering that remains is reputational: the audit itself guesses that CKD and vote are near-separable.
    Two independent auditors, written reasons and a frozen flow table are the published remedy.
  - Unwritten choices also remain: the outcome of a multi-target source, the treatment of scores at 0.995 or
    more, and reserve substitution. Each is closed above.
- **Does the pool represent the fits the shipped default serves?** Only partly, and the report should say so.
  - The frame is curated machine-learning prediction tasks. There is no published frame of the data BART users
    fit, which are observational studies and effect estimation, often at a few hundred rows.
  - Results are conditional on the frame (Boulesteix et al. 2017).
  - The pool lacks rare outcomes, and probably the near-separated band where the move matters.
- **Rows independent, provenance disjoint?** After changes 2 and 3, yes for what passes.
  - No passing dataset shares a source with the seen list. The Statlog heart and Australian credit copies are
    already out.
  - McDermott and Forsyth (2016) confirm that ILPD is distinct from the BUPA liver data, so the audit's
    "Selector" doubt on ILPD is unfounded. UCI's summary says the field records diagnosis. ILPD's remaining
    doubts can be settled from the file, and the survey's tier 2 wrongly omits it.
- **Can 6 and 12 resolve their questions?**
  - R2 resolves per dataset only with a corrected standard error of at most 0.0031. With six datasets, it
    bounds only the share of harmed datasets in the frame, below 0.39 at one-sided 95 percent if none shows
    harm.
  - R3 can show a missed dataset and little else. At 3 of 3 caught, the lower bound on sensitivity is 0.37;
    sparing 2 of 3 gives an interval of 0.09 to 0.99. I checked the survey's figures and they hold.
- **Is the split sound?** Yes as a stratified random allocation on the sealed score, with change 5. R2 and R3
  ask different questions from one frame, and both need the full score range.
- **Would a referee ask for a published suite?** A census of TabArena and CC18 is the published suite. A
  referee would ask for it instead of the plan's hand-made list, and would ask for the sampler papers' sets only
  as well, labeled as seen.

## Claims the study may make

- **From R2:**
  - On the six named datasets, drawn by a fixed rule from a published curated frame, the move's held-out log
    score at the default length is worse than the shipped sampler's by less than 0.01 nats a row, the
    project's own margin, each bound corrected for overlapping splits.
  - Any dataset unresolved at 20 splits is reported as unresolved.
  - With six datasets, a harm that affects most datasets in the frame would likely have shown; nothing more
    general follows.
- **From R3:**
  - If the trigger fires on every resolved dataset that needs the move, the study may say "the trigger missed
    none of the k", with the lower bound on sensitivity.
  - Sparing is descriptive only.
  - "Needs the move" is the study's own definition.
  - Unresolved datasets are counted apart, and fewer than 3 resolved on either side reads unresolved.
- **Not:**
  - any claim about real data in general, or outside the frame;
  - calibration of intervals on real data (coverage is read only under drawn truths);
  - effects (no real effect dataset is scored);
  - probit;
  - that the pool covers the near-separated or rare-outcome fits, unless the sealed scores show it.
