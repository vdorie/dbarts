## Recommendation: major revision

The design's core is right and an improvement on the held-out log score it replaces. The two samplers target one
posterior, so the error of each sampler's estimates against a reference of that posterior, at equal time, is the
direct reading. posteriordb and Margossian and coauthors score samplers this way, and the estimands (means,
spread, tail quantiles of predictive quantities) are the ones posteriordb names. Three defects need a revision
before the list is sealed. As written, the reference's acceptance will almost certainly leave the guard with few
or no accepted datasets. With none accepted the guard reads harmless by vacuous truth. And the margin's argument
and the power claim are partly wrong. Each can be fixed in the plan at little or no compute.

## 1. The reference runs

**1.1 Length and chains.** Sixteen chains, 8 a library, from prior draws, 5,000 sweeps of burn-in, 20,000
recorded at thin 20: 16,000 draws, above posteriordb's 10,000. Starts from the prior are what Vehtari et al. ask
for (diffuse starts). Sound. Two things are lost from the k study's form, which the plan says it reuses. The
length guard is gone: the k study accepted a run only when every check passed at two consecutive block ends, so
that a lucky single block could not pass. And the escalation of the shipped half is gone. The plan should restore
the length guard, which costs nothing when a run passes at once. It should either restore the escalation or say
that it dropped it.

**1.2 Acceptance: R-hat and effective draws are inconsistent, and stricter than the house's own evidence
supports.** Criterion 2 asks for rank-normalized split R-hat below 1.01 over all 16 chains, on k and on the latent
fit at every fitted and every prediction row (up to 5,000 rows). Criterion 3 asks for bulk and tail effective
draws of at least 1,000.

- At stationarity, split R-hat squared is about 1 + m / ESS, with m the number of split chains (here 32). The
  within-chain variance falls short of the posterior variance by about its variance divided by the per-chain
  effective draws, and the pooled estimate does not. At ESS = 1,000 a perfectly stationary reference reads
  R-hat about 1.016. Passing 1.01 needs about 1,600 effective draws, and passing it reliably needs more. The k
  study measured exactly this on its own 16-chain references: a stationary chain "passes 1.01 about a quarter of
  the time at 1,200 effective draws and two-thirds at 1,600 over 16 chains ... and takes about 2,000 to 2,400 to
  pass reliably". Vehtari et al. pair 1.01 with 400 for their four chains (8 split chains, 50 effective draws
  each). Scaled to 32 split chains, the same rule is 1,600, not 1,000. So criterion 3 is not the binding
  criterion: criterion 2 is, and it binds at a precision the plan does not state.
- Requiring it at every row multiplies the failures. The k study used 1.02 at the 99th percentile of rows for this
  reason, and 117 of its 141 failed long runs failed on row R-hat. Margossian et al. (2024, section 1.1) make the
  general point: R-hat at 1.01 measures the chains' mixing, "a separate question" from whether the estimate has
  acceptable error, and reaching it can cost far more than the target precision.
- Requiring k's R-hat reverses a recorded call. The k study read dec-B462's "converge" on the scored quantities
  and on k's quantiles at about 100 effective draws, "not on k's split-Rhat at 1.01, since requiring k to
  converge selects the replications where the shipped kernel already mixes" (decisions.md, the k study's
  calls). Its pilot, which did require k to converge, passed 18 of 52 references. The plan also says k "frames
  nothing" in the guard, yet k gates every reference.
- Expected yield. Under the k study's looser rule, 12 of its 22 real prediction references failed, and 10 of
  the 12 datasets scoring at least 0.8 failed. R2 is stratified by score, so its top blocks will mostly fail
  under the plan's stricter rule, and likely several lower ones too. The plan states no expected count of
  accepted references.

*What the plan should say.* Take the k study's acceptance as written (halves agreeing within 4.5 combined Monte
Carlo standard errors on each scored quantity's mean and 5th and 95th percentiles and on k's seven quantiles;
R-hat at most 1.01 on scalars and 1.02 at the 99th percentile of rows; tail effective draws at least 400 on
scalars and at the 1st percentile of rows; k's effective draws at least 100; k's own R-hat reported), with its
length guard. Add posteriordb's 10,000 draws and the lag-1 report. If the plan keeps R-hat 1.01 at every row, it
must ask for about 2,000 or more effective draws to match it, and state the expected pass rate. Either way, state
the expected number of accepted R2 references, from the k study's rates by score.

**1.3 Can a reference built from both samplers favor either?** In expectation no. The scored fits are
independent of the reference, so the reference's own Monte Carlo error adds the same amount to both expected
errors and cancels from D, as the plan says. Bias is another matter. Acceptance tolerates a disagreement between
the halves of up to about 4.5 times the combined standard error, about 0.2 to 0.28 posterior sd at 500 to 1,000
effective draws a half (R-hat at 1.01 tolerates about the same). The pooled reference can then sit up to about
0.1 to 0.14 sd toward one half, which is 1 to 2 units in the guard's scale, the size of the whole margin. If a
sampler's short fits are biased the same way as its own long half, the reference favors that sampler. When that
sampler is the shipped one, the guard is conservative. When it is the move (for instance, slower tree mixing on
some rows), the guard is anti-conservative. The plan's argument that the reference is "never the sampler under
test alone" does not cover this residual.

*What the plan should say.* State the acceptance tolerance in the guard's units. Add a sensitivity reading at no
compute: D computed against each library's half alone as well as against the pooled reference, with any
harmless reading that does not hold against both halves flagged. Alternatively, score each library's fits
against the other library's half.

**1.4 The vacuous pass.** "The guard reads harmless when every row estimand and class reads harmless on every R2
dataset with an accepted reference." With no reference accepted, that reads harmless. Given 1.2, this outcome is
likely. *Required:* a minimum number of accepted references for the guard to read at all (I suggest 3 of 6);
below it the guard is unresolved and reported with its counts. Rule 2 of step 2 must say that an unresolved guard
blocks the recommendation as an unresolved class does.

## 2. Estimands, error measure, equal-time pairing and the no-harm bar

**2.1 Estimands and error.** The estimands are natural. posteriordb names means, variances and tail quantiles of
parameters and predictive quantities. The unit for a posterior mean is Margossian et al.'s "expected squared
error attained with 100 independent samples". The plan honestly calls the same unit for the sd and the interval
ends its own. Two attributions need correcting:
- The non-inferiority use of one unit as a margin on a difference between samplers is the study's own.
  Margossian et al. use Var/100 as a precision target for one estimator, not as a margin.
- The 1-Wasserstein reading "scaled by the reference's standard deviation ... as Zhang et al. 2022 read it" is
  not Zhang et al.'s. Zhang et al. compute W1 on the whole unconstrained parameter vector from 100 draws, and
  scale each model's distances by the median distance of single-path Pathfinder (section III.1). A per-row
  univariate W1 scaled by the reference sd is the study's own.

Joint estimands are absent: the band (a simultaneous interval) and a one-value functional such as the average
predicted probability over rows. A sampler with the right marginals but the wrong dependence across rows would
pass. The guard can report the reference mass inside each fit's band at no compute.

**2.2 Equal-time pairing.** r is the median ratio of full default fits, and the shipped arm gets ceil(1000 r) -
500 draws. Time that does not scale with sweeps (setup, cut points, model frame) pulls r toward 1. The shipped
arm then gets fewer sweeps than equal time would give it: the needed count is 1000 c_move / c_shipped, at least
1000 r. That favors the move, which is anti-conservative for a no-harm guard. The effect is probably small at
these sizes. The plan already measures seconds per sweep from 100-sweep segments. It should either set the
shipped arm's draws from the per-sweep ratio, or report the share of fixed time in each dataset's timing. Welch's
unpaired comparison is right, since the two libraries' streams cannot share random numbers. The errors are
chi-square-like and skewed; at 20 a library a bootstrap bound beside Welch's would show whether skew matters.

**2.3 The margin.** One unit is defensible as a usability bar: Monte Carlo error under a tenth of a posterior sd
is small beside the posterior's own uncertainty, and this matches the house's absolute bars (dec-B455). But the
plan's coverage equivalence is right only for the mean:
- Mean: one unit is a displacement of 0.1 sd, coverage 0.898. Confirmed (exact: 0.8983).
- sd: one unit is 7 percent too narrow, coverage 0.8735. That is a loss of 0.027, not "about equal" to 0.02. A
  loss of 0.02 corresponds to 0.6 units.
- Interval ends, which the guard also scores: the 5th percentile of 100 normal draws has variance 0.0447
  sd^2, so one unit is a systematic shift of 0.21 sd. Both ends shifted inward by that much give coverage 0.848, a
  loss of 0.052. A loss of 0.02 corresponds to about 0.18 units. So on interval ends the margin is about 2.5
  times looser than the house's coverage bar.

*What the plan should say.* Correct the margin paragraph with these three equivalences, and justify the single
margin as a Monte Carlo precision target, not as a match to the 0.02 coverage bar. Alternatively, set
per-estimand margins (about 0.6 units on the sd, 0.2 on the interval ends) if matching the coverage bar is the
intent. That costs power.

**2.4 The power claim, rederived.** se(D) = s (1/20 + 1/20)^(1/2) = 0.316 s, correct. With t at 38 degrees of
freedom (2.024), P(resolve | D = 0) = Phi(1 / (0.316 s) - 2.024) gives 0.993, 0.910, 0.872 and 0.587 at s =
0.71, 0.94, 1 and 1.41, matching the plan. s at most 141 / E on a mean (100 / E times the sd of a chi-square
with one degree of freedom) is correct, as is 0.91 at E = 150. The upper bound of 1 - 0.05^(1/6) = 0.39 on the
harmed share is correct for 0 harms of 6.

Two aggregate claims are wrong:
- "All six resolve with probability 0.96, 0.57, 0.44 and 0.04" counts one test a dataset. The guard is a
  conjunction over five row estimands times up to three row groupings (all, interior, near 0 or 1) on each
  dataset, about 15 a dataset and 90 in all. Strongly correlated within a dataset, but not one test. Even at 5
  effectively independent tests a dataset, the joint probability at s = 0.94 is 0.91^30 = 0.06, not 0.57.
  Interval ends rest on tail effective draws and have the largest s, so they will usually decide it.
- The 0.39 bound and the six-dataset power both assume six accepted references (see 1.2), and the six are a
  score-stratified draw conditioned on acceptance, not a random sample of the frame. The bound holds for
  accepted datasets like these.

*What the plan should say.* Restate the guard's expected reading as step 2's "Expected reading" paragraph does:
the expected number of accepted references, and the probability that the whole conjunction resolves at D = 0
for a stated within-dataset correlation. Make the first dataset's measurement of s an internal pilot that
re-estimates the variance blind to D: if any s exceeds 0.94, every dataset gets 40 fits a library (call 15's
alternative, about 0.1 core-hour a dataset). That rule must be written before any fit.

**2.5 Can a sampler that mixes worse pass?** Yes, by design, in four ways the plan should state plainly:
1. The margin is absolute. A move with a quarter of the shipped sampler's row effective draws (errors 0.2
   against 0.8, D = 0.6) passes whenever s is under about 0.6, as class averages over many rows will be. posteriordb names effective draws per second as "the central implementation-dependent
   performance metric", and the ratio of mean errors is exactly that. The plan reports the ratio and frames
   nothing on it. At 20 fits a library a ratio bar of 1.25 would seldom resolve (with a coefficient of variation
   of 0.5 over fits, the bound on a true ratio of 1.1 is about 1.5), so I do not ask for it as a bar. The report
   should give the ratio with its interval on every estimand, and flag any whose lower bound passes 1.25, the
   cost bar of dec-B620 restated as accuracy.
2. k is outside the guard.
3. Datasets whose references fail are not read at all. That is where the samplers differ most, though not where
   harm from the move is expected.
4. Joint estimands are not scored (2.1).

**2.6 Multiplicity.** "Harmless" on every estimand, class and dataset is an intersection-union reading. Each test
must reject on its own, and the joint claim keeps the per-test level, so no correction is needed. The plan is
right there, but power falls (2.4). "Harm shown" is a union, and it does need a rule:
- In the guard, harm needs D at least 1 with its lower bound above 0. At D = 0 that is P(Z >= 1 / (0.316 s)):
  0.0004 at s = 0.94 and 0.0125 at s = 1.41 a test, across about 90 correlated tests.
- In step 2's rule 2 (outside this referee's brief, but the same question), harm needs the worsening at 0.02 or
  more with z at least 2.81, at most 0.0025 a look and 0.005 over two looks. That applies to each of about 50
  class-weighting cells. That gives a Bonferroni bound near 0.09 for a false harm somewhere in A, which
  recommends parking.

*What the plan should say.* A harm shown frames the recommendation only after it is confirmed on fresh seeds
named before the rerun, as step 1 confirms its failures (about 0.1 core-hour a guard dataset). Alternatively, the
report states the expected number of false harms at a true zero and a family-wise level for the harm family.

## 3. Dropping the held-out log score

What is lost is as the plan says: a reading on datasets whose references fail, in predictive units users know,
comparable with Chipman, George and McCulloch (2010). One argument against keeping it is wrong, though. The
Nadeau and Bengio correction, and the 0.0054-nat requirement that follows from it, belong to a claim about
generalization over splits. The paired question here is whether two samplers of one posterior predict
differently on one fixed split. That needs only the 20 fits a library already planned, read by Welch's test
like D. The fits already predict at the 20 percent prediction rows, so the score costs nothing but reading
those rows' outcomes after the seal, which the held-out rule allows. Nothing in the guard's logic or in step 3
needs it: step 3 reads reference mass. But it is the only real-outcome reading on datasets whose references
fail.

*Recommendation (a call, with its alternative).* Keep the prediction rows' outcomes sealed until the guard's
reading is committed, then report the held-out log score and Brier score differences per dataset with Welch
intervals, framing nothing. They matter most where references fail. Alternative: drop them as the plan does,
and say the failed-reference datasets get no real-outcome reading.

## 4. The screen

**4.1 Can the dropped criteria bias the paired comparison?** No criterion, dropped or kept, can favor one exact
sampler over the other on a given dataset: every one is a property of the data, and both samplers target the
same posterior. What the criteria change is the population of posteriors and the share of references that pass.
Taking each in turn:
- *Independent rows, case-control sampling, duplicates, time-to-event status*: agreed; no bias. The posterior is
  well defined and both samplers target it.
- *Leaks*: no bias, but the argument as written is wrong. The blocks are ranks of the pool's scores, so the draw
  fixes R2's share of each rank sextile, not of each score range. If leaks raise many datasets toward
  separation, the top two or three sextiles are all near-separated, and R2's near-separated share equals the
  pool's. Leaks therefore can raise R2's share of near-separated data, and with it the share of references that
  fail. This costs power, not validity. *Correct the sentence.*
- *Kept criteria 1 and 2* (outcome as the source defined it, real data) cannot bias the comparison either. They
  are criteria of population, of which posteriors users meet. The section should say that the screen keeps
  population and feasibility criteria and drops none that bear on bias, since none does.
- *Another criterion, in effect added by section 4*: complete-case deletion. "A column more than half missing is
  dropped, then rows with any missing covariate." This is not what a user's fit does: bart()'s default is
  na.action = na.keepPredictors, which keeps the rows. It can also cut rows heavily. OpenML lists 75,244 of
  APSFailure's 76,000 rows with a missing value, 75 of hepatitis's 155 (leaving about 80, below the 100-row
  floor), and 263 of cylinder-bands' 540. Criterion 4's floors are read from listings before this step, so a
  passer can fall below them unseen. The minority share cannot be rechecked without reading the outcome.
  *Required:* fit with the package's default missing-predictor handling and drop only columns entirely missing
  (or above a stated high missing share). Otherwise re-apply the row floor after deletion and state that the
  share is not rechecked.

**4.2 Counts.** The flow table's arithmetic holds in every column: 151 - 27 - 11 = 113 entering; failures 22 +
24 + 10 + 10 + 20 + 1 = 87; 26 pass. Stage by stage it gives 9, 6 and 11 passers, and 9, 4 and 4 with at least
1,000 rows (17), matching the passers' listed sizes. Fields total 7 + 4 + 11 + 4 = 26. The orchestrator's count
of 43 with 24 large, reconciled in section 5, is not checkable without the per-entry table.

## 5. Sharing the references with step 3

The condition (step 3's trigger form committed before any R2 reading is opened) is necessary but not enough:
- "Form" must mean the selected trigger itself, its feature and threshold computed from step 2's simulated fits,
  not only the candidate set and selection rule. Step 2's report must wait for that commit whether or not step 3
  ever runs.
- Step 2's report shows, for R2, the reference mass inside each fit's interval (and readings without the scores
  of 0.995 or more), which is step 3's need-the-move reading. The maintainer decides whether to run step 3 after
  seeing it. So every element of step 3 must also be frozen before R2 opens: the need-the-move rule, the pass
  rule (fire on every needing dataset, spare at least half), the fewer-than-3 rule and R3's size. Any later
  amendment of step 3 drops R2 from its test.
- The trigger selection is mechanical. Knowing the R2 and R3 datasets' rank blocks from the committed assignment
  therefore cannot steer it, provided the rule leaves no discretion. Say so, or commit the assignment as
  unordered sets.

With those three, sharing is honest, and 18 datasets is better than 12.

## 6. The seeded draw and the reserves

The code is sound. Base R's RNG kinds are pinned, it uses `i[sample.int(length(i), 1L)]` (avoiding `sample()` on
a vector of length one), and vapply's integer type matches. At N = 26 the R2 blocks hold 4, 4, 5, 4, 4 and 5
(ceiling(6 r / 26)). At M = 20 the R3 blocks hold 1 or 2, leaving 8 reserves, as stated. Exact block boundaries
(78 / 26 and 60 / 20) are exact in floating point. Five things are unspecified or wrong:
1. Reserves are "drawn from the same block" with no seed or order given, and an R2 block's other members may be
   in R3. Specify the pool (the eight unassigned only) and the rule (e.g. the reserve nearest in rank within the
   same R2 or R3 block, else the next block's), in code.
2. The 80/20 split and the rpart 5-fold assignment for the separation score have no seeds or code. Write them out
   as the allocation is.
3. The stage-4 order file's checksum is truncated ("f8978e03...b423d5"). A sealed rule needs the full digest.
4. R3's size (12, or every passer not in R2) is still the maintainer's call. It must be settled before the seal,
   since the draw's code depends on it.
5. The draw needs the scores of all 26 before the assignment, so the pre-fit check, and any shortfall to stage 4,
   must complete first. Say so in section 6's order.

## 7. Compute

The arithmetic is consistent with the plan's own rates. References: 6 x 16 x 25,000 chain-sweeps at 3.75 ms is
2.5 core-hours, 3.4 with 35 percent. Fits: 348 at 4,000 chain-sweeps is 1.45 core-hours at 3.75 ms, about 2 with
overhead; "about 1" implies an average near 2.6 ms, plausible since R2 spans 124 to 4,000 fitted rows. Step 2
totals 20.0. The whole plan totals 40.2 expected, 46 with step 3 and about 75 at most, above the stop of 69. The
rate of 3.75 ms is itself unverified (Not verified says so). The fixes above add little: the length guard and
cross-half scoring add nothing. Escalation of the shipped half to 100,000 sweeps adds about 0.7 core-hour a
dataset that escalates, up to about 4. A second set of 20 fits on every dataset adds about 0.6. Confirmation
reruns add about 0.1 a dataset. Memory: full draws at up to 5,000 rows are about 0.6 GB a reference for the
latent fit alone, so they should be compacted after scoring, as the k study compacted.
