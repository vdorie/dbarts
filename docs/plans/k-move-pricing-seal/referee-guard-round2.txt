## Recommendation: minor revision

The revision takes every first-round point, most in full, and the design now argues as published sampler
comparisons do. What is left is cheap to fix but real. One is a rule that cannot work as written: the length
guard's first check has too few draws to pass. One is a pilot threshold sized to a single test, not to the
conjunction it guards, so the guard is underpowered over a range of s. One is a missing rule: where a confirmed
harm on R2 sends the recommendation. One is a provenance rule whose wording and screen file disagree on spect.
The expected compute also leaves out the escalations it names. None needs new compute beyond about a core-hour,
and none changes the design's core.

## 2. New defects in the reference rule

**2.1 The length guard's first check cannot pass.** A reference is accepted only when every check passes at two
consecutive block ends, and the first job's checks read at 12,000 and 16,000 recorded sweeps. At thin 20 over 16
chains, 12,000 sweeps hold 9,600 draws, under criterion 1's 10,000. So no reference can be accepted at 16,000.
Every reference runs to at least 20,000 recorded (25,000 sweeps), and the "sixth less" saving never occurs. The
k study's form (first job 8,000, checks at 4,000 and 8,000) had no draw-count criterion. *What the plan should
say:* criterion 1 is read at the accepting (later) block end only, and checks 2 to 5 at both ends. That keeps the
guard's intent at no cost. Alternative: a first job of 20,000 with checks at 16,000 and 20,000.

**2.2 Acceptance, three loose ends.**
- *One-value estimands.* Criteria 3 and 4 apply R-hat 1.01 and 400 tail draws "for every one-value estimand". The
  guard's scored estimands are all row estimands. Its one-value quantities are k (which criterion 5 treats apart)
  and the average probability over the prediction rows, which frames nothing. Name the set. If it is the average
  probability, a scalar R-hat of 1.01 over 16 chains again binds as a precision demand (the k study's quarter at
  1,200 draws), on a quantity that frames nothing.
- *Agreement at every row.* Criterion 2 tests agreement at 4.5 combined standard errors on every row's mean and two
  quantiles. At up to 5,000 rows and two row quantities that is about 30,000 tests. At stationarity, with exact
  standard errors, P(any |z| > 4.5) is about 30,000 x 6.8e-6 = 0.2, and more with noisy quantile standard errors.
  The k study's own record gives a sound run's chance failure of agreement as 2 to 22 percent at its sizes. R2's
  sizes run larger, so the projected yield is optimistic for the large datasets. State the chance failure rate
  at R2's row counts. Alternative: read agreement at the 99th percentile of rows, as R-hat is.
- *The yield's basis.* The k study's real references carried readings under both links. Say whether "12 of 22
  failed" is logistic acceptance alone. If acceptance there was joint over links, the logistic rate is at least
  as high, and the projection is conservative.
- Wording: criterion 2's "every scored estimand's mean and 5th and 95th percentiles" should read "every scored
  quantity's" (the latent fit and the probability at each row). An estimand such as the posterior sd has no
  percentiles.

## 3. The numbers, rederived

**3.1 Coverage equivalents of the margin.** All confirmed (normal posterior, 90 percent interval):
- *Mean:* one unit is 0.1 sd, coverage 0.8983. A 0.02 loss takes 0.344 sd, 11.8 units.
- *sd:* one unit is 7.1 percent narrow, coverage 0.8735. A 0.02 loss is 0.59 units.
- *Ends:* the 5th percentile of 100 draws has variance 0.04466 sd^2, so one unit is 0.211 sd. Both ends inward
  give 0.8483, and a 0.02 loss is 0.18 units.

One refinement the plan may add. These figures treat one unit as a systematic error. Coverage loss is quadratic
in a mean's displacement, so the mean's 0.898 holds however the squared error splits between bias and noise. For
the sd and the ends it does not. A unit of zero-mean noise costs much less: coverage 0.8977 on the sd and 0.8924
on the ends (simulated). So the ends' 0.848 is the worst case, all bias and inward.

**3.2 Power.** One test at 20 fits a library, P = Phi(1 / (0.316 s) - 2.024): 0.992, 0.910, 0.872 and 0.586 at
s = 0.71, 0.94, 1 and 1.41. At 40 fits (t 1.991): 0.997 at 0.94, 0.881 at 1.41. Conjunction at 5 effective tests a
dataset, s = 0.94: 0.242 over 3 datasets and 0.059 over 6 at 20 fits; 0.958 and 0.918 at 40. All match the plan.
The false harm a test, 1 - Phi(1 / (0.316 s)), is 0.00038 at s = 0.94 and 0.0124 at 1.41, matching. The bounds
1 - 0.05^(1/a) are 0.393 at 6 and 0.632 at 3, matching. Harm at twice the margin shows with probability 0.999 a run
at s = 1, matching.

**3.3 Accepted references.** I assume the plan's model: pass rates 2/12 at a score of 0.8 or more and 8/10 below,
10/22 of the pool below 0.8, assigned by rank to the blocks of 4, 4, 5, 4, 4 and 5 at N = 26. Then:
- At R2 = 6: 2.75 expected accepted, P(at least 3) = 0.61, P(none) = 0.008. This matches 2.7, 0.6 and 0.01.
- At R2 = 9: 4.21 expected and P(at least 3) = 0.93, against the plan's 4.1 and 0.92. The plan's 4.1 is the
  unstratified 9 x 0.4545. Immaterial.

The projection is soft, and the plan should say so. Under Jeffreys uncertainty in the three rates, P(at least 3)
has a 10th to 90th percentile range of 0.29 to 0.87 at 6 and 0.69 to 0.99 at 9. If the pool holds more datasets
scoring 0.8 or more than the k study did (75 percent), it is 0.30 at 6 and 0.68 at 9. Both favor 9.

The figure call 7 needs is the joint one: the chance the guard reads harmless at a true D of 0. That is P(at
least 3 accepted) times the conjunction over the accepted datasets (5 effective tests each, joint.R):

| R2 | fits a library | s = 0.70 | s = 0.94 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 20 | 0.55 | 0.13 |
| 6 | 40 | 0.61 | 0.58 |
| 9 | 20 | 0.81 | 0.13 |
| 9 | 40 | 0.93 | 0.88 |

So 9 datasets at 40 fits is the design that reads, and 6 at 20 rarely does.

**3.4 Compute against the plan's own rates.** 3.75 ms a chain-sweep, a 35 percent overhead, 0.08 core-hours for 40
default fits.
- *A reference:* 16 x 25,000 sweeps is 0.42 core-hours, 0.56 with overhead (0.47 at 21,000). An escalation
  (8 chains of 80,000) is 0.67, 0.9 with overhead. "13 minutes" and "25 more" on 2 cores are right.
- *R2 references:* the "8.8 at most" (3.375 + 6 x 0.9) and R3's "17.6 at most" (6.75 + 12 x 0.9) are right. The
  expected figures leave out the escalations they name. R2's "about 3 (one escalation)" is 3.1 to 3.4 before the
  escalation and 4.0 to 4.3 with it. R3's "about 6.3 (two escalations)" is 6.3 to 6.75 before and 8.1 to 8.6 with
  them. With 2.1 as written, no reference stops at 21,000, so the upper ends hold.
- *Totals:* the guard is about 6.0 expected, not 4.9. Through step 2 it is about 43, not 42. With step 3 it is
  about 52, not 49, and the stop at 1.5 times is about 78, not 73. The maximum of 98 rederives exactly
  (5 + 17 + 19 + 13.4 + 2 + 23.3 + 17.6 + 1). The R2-at-9 increment (1.6 references, 0.9 fits) is right on the
  plan's basis: 1.7 at the 25,000-sweep rate.
- *Memory:* 0.6 GB a reference is confirmed (12,800 to 16,000 draws x 5,000 rows x 8 bytes).

## 4. What is new

**4.1 The internal pilot.**
- *Error rates.* For every dataset but the pilot's, the fit count is fixed by data independent of that dataset's
  own fits, so those tests keep their level exactly. Only the pilot dataset's own test is re-sized on its own
  data. I simulated it at the margin (true D = 1, harmless when Welch's upper bound is under 1, 20 raised to 40
  when either library's s passes 0.94). With normal errors, the pilot's rate is 0.026, 0.030 and 0.024 at s =
  0.7, 0.94 and 1.2, against 0.025 nominal (Monte Carlo se 0.0018): at most a few thousandths of inflation, worst
  with the threshold at the true s. So the pilot keeps its error rates to within that. The pilot is "blind to D"
  only in the sense that it computes no difference. It reads each library's spread, and for errors whose spread
  tracks their mean that spread nearly reveals each mean. That is harmless only because the rule's sole output is
  the fit count and the rule is mechanical. Say both.
- *The threshold is sized to the wrong test.* s = 0.94 is where one test resolves 0.91 (my first-round
  suggestion, and my error there). The conjunction needs a per-test 0.993 to 0.9965, which at 20 fits means s up
  to 0.71 over 3 datasets and 0.67 over 6. As written, a pilot that reads s between 0.7 and 0.94 keeps 20 fits,
  where the conjunction resolves 0.06 to 0.9. Every dataset goes to 40 only past 0.94, where at 40 it resolves
  0.92 at s = 0.95 but 0.02 at s = 1.41.
- *The expected reading contradicts the rule.* "Resolves harmless with probability about 0.9 or more if the
  pilot's 40 fits hold s at 0.94 or less" describes a branch the rule never takes: 40 fits are taken only when
  some s passes 0.94.
- *One dataset sizes all.* R2 spans 124 to 4,000 fitted rows, so its s on one dataset says little about another's.

*What the plan should say.* Take 40 fits a library from the start. Call 15 prices that at about 0.6 core-hour,
and it removes the pilot dataset's re-sizing question. Keep an internal pilot per dataset, reading that dataset's
own spreads after its 40 fits and raising it to 80 when any s passes 0.95. 0.95 is the threshold at which 40 fits
give the conjunction 0.9 over 6 datasets (1.0 over 3). Restate the expected reading from the table in 3.3.
Alternative, the plan's form with the threshold set from the conjunction: 20 fits up to s = 0.67, 40 up to 0.95,
and 80 past it.

**4.2 Skew, and the second bound.** Each fit's error is a sum of squared errors, chi-square-like. Where rows move
together, as a shared k error makes them, it is nearly one chi-square. I simulated the case where a fit's error is
gamma (mean 2 for the move, 1 for the shipped, true D = 1) at 20 fits. Welch's one-sided 0.025 bound then reads
harmless at a rate of:
- 0.053 at shape 0.5;
- 0.033 to 0.044 at shape 2 (two simulations);
- 0.025 at shape 8.

The percentile bootstrap is worse (0.069, 0.043 and 0.032). Since the percentile bound is the narrower one, "both"
equals Welch in every replication (boot.R). A studentized bootstrap helps at moderate skew (0.029 at shape 2) but
not at shape 0.5 (0.065). At 40 fits Welch is 0.036 at shape 2. So the second bound adds nothing as written, and
the guard's per-test level can be about twice nominal where the errors are most skewed.

*What the plan should say.* Drop the percentile bootstrap as a second requirement, or replace it with a
studentized one. State the level at the measured skew: each library's skewness of the error over fits, read in
the same blind pass. 40 fits halves the excess, another reason for 4.1's recommendation.

**4.3 Fresh-seed confirmation.** Sound for what it can catch. The rerun's fits are independent of the first run's,
so a false harm at a true 0 needs both runs: about 0.0004 x 0.0004 a test at s = 0.94. An unconfirmed harm reads
unresolved, which still blocks the move. So confirmation can never manufacture a harmless reading. Three gaps:
- *The shared reference.* The rerun is scored against the same reference. A reference that leans toward the
  shipped half (tolerated up to 1 to 2 units, 1.3 of the plan) shows the move worse in both runs, and
  confirmation does not remove that. Call 22's per-half reading flags only harmless readings. *Should say:* a
  guard harm stands only when it also holds against the move's own half, the half that can only favor the move.
  Symmetrically, the flag on a harmless reading keys on the shipped half.
- *Where a confirmed guard harm goes.* "After the reading" maps harm in A to parking and harm only in B to step 3.
  A confirmed harm on R2 is in neither, so the rule is silent. *Should say*, my recommendation: it blocks "on for
  every logistic fit" and recommends step 3. Real datasets that pass acceptance are mostly the fits with no
  strong signal, which is what B stands for. The alternative is to treat it as a harm in A (parking).
- *The stated rate, and the rerun's count.* Rule 2 still gives the unconfirmed family bound, about 0.09 in A.
  Add the confirmed one: at most 50 x 0.005 x 0.0025, about 0.0006. The rerun should run at the count the harm was
  read at (40 if the guard went to 40, a regime's full count if it showed there), not at the first look's.

**4.4 Equal time per sweep.** Right, and as asked. Subtracting a one-sweep fit's time leaves 999 sweeps' cost per
library. Both libraries pay the same fixed time, so the shipped arm's ceil(1000 r) sweeps fill the time the move's
1,000 take. Two small points. Time the guard's fits with the prediction rows, as they are scored, since prediction
cost scales with the draws kept. And E3 is still timed by total fit time. That is right if its per-sweep R work is
counted as per-sweep, but it then includes the fixed time once, pulling r slightly toward 1. Use E3's remainder
too.

**4.5 At least 3 accepted references, or unresolved.** Right: it closes the vacuous pass, and rule 2 reads it as an
unresolved class. Name the 3 as the study's own (no published rule sets it). Say that 0 harms of 3 bounds the
harmed share among such datasets only below 0.63. And add the joint figure of 3.3 to call 7. A guard that reads
13 to 58 percent of the time at 6 datasets is mostly a report of counts.

   spect on criterion 3 because spectf fails criterion 5. Likewise, rice passes because Pumpkin_Seeds (same source,

   and the blocks.

5. *Length guard and criterion 1* (2.1), since the rule cites the reference's acceptance for step 3's
