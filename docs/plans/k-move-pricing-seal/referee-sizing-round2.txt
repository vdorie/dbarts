## 2. The guard's reading and R2 = 12

**Operating characteristics.** At 0 accepted datasets the guard is unresolved. At 1, that dataset's own reading
stands and the guard is unresolved. At 2 or more, it reads harmless only when every dataset does and the t does. That
is the right ladder. Under a harm confined to one dataset, the intersection over datasets controls the level at
each dataset's own level, whatever the spread across datasets. The t governs only the claim about the mean, and its
caveat under skew is stated. At a true 0 the guard reads harmless about 0.05 of the time at 2 accepted and about
0.96 at 3 (my round 1 figures). With true D = 1 on every dataset it never does.

**Rederivation** (6,000 draws; acceptance cells (high, large) 0/5, (high, small) 2/7, (low, large) 1/2,
(low, small) 7/8; high-score share by size 5/7 and 7/15; 14 of 24 large; loss 0.15):

| R2 | mine | plan | t alone | spread 0.25 by estimand | by dataset |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 0.75 | 0.75 | 0.76 | 0.45 | 0.53 |
| 12 | 0.83 | 0.83 | 0.85 | 0.52 | 0.59 |
| 14 | 0.90 | 0.90 | 0.92 | 0.58 | 0.66 |
| 16 | 0.93 | 0.93 | 0.95 | 0.59 | 0.67 |

Power at the 0.2 quantile of the inputs at 12: 0.73 (plan 0.74). R2 = 12 is right on the stated rule, and the
feasibility framing is right: assurance tracks P(at least 3 accepted) to within 0.01. I did not check the claim that
"every one of those failures failed row R-hat", which rests on the k study's record.

**Under spread, no size reaches 0.8.** Confirmed: it plateaus near 0.6. The reason the plan gives is right: every
added dataset must read harmless alone. But the plan should also name the lever that does move it, which is fits a
dataset, not datasets. At R2 = 12 with a spread of 0.25, 80 fits a library give 0.53, 160 give 0.70 and 320 give
0.74. With a spread of 0.25 some true D sit near 0.6 to 0.75, close enough to the margin that no plausible count
resolves them. The plan's choice to report this scenario and not size on it is acceptable for a non-inferiority
design sized at no difference. The plan should add one sentence giving the 160-fit figure, so that a reader can see
the plateau comes from the scenario, not from the design.

## 3. R3 and step 3 as refutation plus estimates

**Counts.** Whole frame of 36 to 45: I get 1.9 to 2.4 resolving as needing and 8.4 to 10.6 as not, with none
needing 0.27 to 0.21 of the time. That matches the plan ("about one time in four"). The 35 to 45 passers rest on 3
to 5 of 80 census draws, whose own uncertainty is wider (3 of 80 has an exact interval of about 1 to 11 percent). A
range is the honest statement, and the plan gives one.

**Refutation rates.** My rates match the plan's within about 0.04 once one thing is counted: a miss refutes only
when its confirming rerun reads the dataset as needing again, which the plan's model gives probability 0.9.
size7.R refutes on any spared needing dataset without that rerun. With it, at 36 and 45 passers:

| trigger | refuted, mine | plan |
|---|---|---|
| right (sensitivity 1, specificity 0.75) | 0.000 | essentially never |
| misses 1 in 10 | 0.15 to 0.18 | 0.16 to 0.19 |
| misses half | 0.49 to 0.56 | 0.54 to 0.60 |
| spares 3 in 10 | 0.12 to 0.16 | 0.12 to 0.16 |

So a trigger missing half escapes 0.44 to 0.51 of the time, against the reported 0.40 to 0.46. The conclusion, "a
bad trigger escapes about half the time", stands; the figures should be restated with the confirmation counted.
The refutation rule itself is sound. A confirmed miss is a valid refutation at any count. The specificity rule
(exact one-sided 0.025 upper bound below one half) refutes only when at most about 1 of 9 is spared, a weak but
correctly stated test.

**New point, to fix before step 3 is committed: call 34's in-sample sensitivity.** Once the real data can only
refute, a trigger that is not refuted "recommends the move on where it fires, resting on step 2's sensitivity". That
sensitivity is read on the same simulated fits that selected the trigger. The selection keeps the bound on spared
gain under 0.02 in every A cell, a constraint that pushes the chosen candidate toward firing on those same fits. Its
in-sample sensitivity is therefore optimistic, and this is now the only positive evidence for the recommendation.
The standard remedy for a selected rule is to estimate its accuracy on data that did not choose it. The plan's
alternative, selecting on half and reading on the other half, does that, but it halves the selection's data. Better
is a fresh set of simulated replications of the A cells (and B's, for specificity), drawn after the trigger is
committed and read only for its sensitivity and specificity. At half the A regimes' first-look sizes that costs
about 1 core-hour. Labeling the in-sample figure does not cure it, because the recommendation reads it as evidence.

## 5. Buderer (1996)

The plan can stand without it. It now cites Buderer for one remark only: that a confirmatory bound on sensitivity
is sized by the count with the condition over its prevalence, so hundreds of datasets at a prevalence near 0.05.
That remark frames no reading, since step 3 no longer has a confirmatory pass. Bujang and Adnan, which the author
read, establish it alone: their tables give N1 / prevalence and 980 at 5 percent. The citation is honestly marked
"Not read: cited as Bujang and Adnan give it". The remark should be attributed to Bujang and Adnan, naming Buderer
only as the origin they give, so that nothing is stated from a paper no one read. Fetching it (the maintainer's
access; scratch/papers/buderer-1996.pdf, doi 10.1111/j.1553-2712.1996.tb03538.x) would be tidy but is not a seal
condition.
