\section{Changes the design needs}\label{sec:changes}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{One density rule.} The parabola contour through the saddle point (Algorithm~\ref{alg:dens},
Theorem~\ref{thm:dens}) serves $f$ and $q$ at every shape and $x$; the split at $b=12$, the Talbot contour and its
error estimate go. The left series is optional at small $x$. The vertical line through the saddle point is valid at
every $b$ but its integrability argument as written holds only for $b>1$, and its cost grows like $b^{-2}$.
\item \textbf{The ratio in tilted, cancellation-free form.} $r=f(\omega')q(\omega)/(f(\omega)q(\omega'))$ from
$\log f$ and $\log q$ evaluated by Proposition~\ref{prop:cf}. The untilted form ($\fz$ and $\qt$) the first version
proposed puts the rounding floor at up to $10^{-7}$ per step at the largest shapes; the tilted form, with the
lead in double-double, at about \FLOORSTEP{} at the pins (Section~\ref{sec:floor}). $q$ is evaluated at both points
each sweep, as designed. $f$ is evaluated fresh at the proposal only: the current point's $\log f$ is the cached
value moved to the new $\psi$ by the exact update \eqref{eq:tiltupd} (item 7). So a step makes three density
evaluations and one update, the design's count, when the row's previous step left a valid entry; after a fresh draw
or any other write to $\omega_i$ it makes four (item 7). Without the update the tilted form would always need four,
since $\psi_i$ changes every sweep.

figure is measured at the pins before it is quoted (item 17); and a build that stops at double precision is exact only in the sense of
Section~\ref{sec:ideal} (Proposition~\ref{prop:fixedtop}); which sense the package adopts is

\section{For the maintainer}\label{sec:maint}

Two questions came out of the review. Neither is settled in the theorems: each theorem says which idealization
it needs, and the answers below decide what the package promises.

\subsection*{Question 1: what does ``exact'' mean once the arithmetic is floating point?}

\textbf{Background.} Every sampler in the package, today's exact Polya-Gamma sum included, is exact in one sense
only: if its random numbers were perfect real numbers and its arithmetic had no rounding, its long-run
distribution would be the posterior. On a computer each random number is one of $2^{32}$ values, and each
accept-or-reject test inside a sampler can come out differently from the ideal test, with probability up to about
one in four billion. Today's sum makes two or more such tests for each unit of shape, so a draw at shape 64
carries up to about one chance in twenty to thirty million of differing from its ideal, and nobody counts these. The new step has
the same kind of error from its own random draws, plus one of its own: its accept-or-keep decision uses densities
computed with a proven error bound, and when the decision is too close to call within that bound at the highest
precision built, the step has to do something. The first version of the density formula left that case at up to
about one step in ten million at the largest shapes. The revised formula, with one number per point (the lead
term) computed in double-double, leaves it at \FLOORSTEPWORDS{} at every point tested; the engine's own figure
is measured when it is built, and in plain double that one number alone would raise the figure to about one step
in a few billion.

\textbf{Options.}
\begin{itemize}
\item[A.] \emph{The package's standard.} The step is exact in the same sense as every other sampler: under perfect
arithmetic, with its floor stated. The build uses the revised density formula in double precision with rigorous
error bounds, the lead term and the cached density in double-double, keeps the old value when the decision is too
close to call, and counts those cases. Build effort: the evaluator as designed, the error bookkeeping written once
(about the size of the evaluator itself), and double-double versions, with error bounds, of five real functions
(exp, log, log1p, sine, square root) for the lead and the cache; no new dependency. Run time: three density
evaluations a step (four after a fresh draw), each far costlier than the design's sizing assumed. With the error
bound as specified the step is slower than today's sum at every shape when $|\psi|$ is large, and pays only at
shapes of a few hundred and up when $|\psi|$ is small; a cheaper bound (proven, not built) could make it pay from
about a thousand trials. S1 times it and sets the rule from the timings (Section~\ref{sec:changes}, item 8). The
double-double part is a few hundred operations for $f$'s lead and the update, plus one double-double log1p per
term of each $q$ lead ($2(K+1)$ a step), small against the certificate. What a user would see: a count of close
calls, expected to be zero in practice. The documentation quotes the engine's measured floor. At the scripts'
floor, $6.2\times10^{-12}$ a step, about one fit in 1600 of $10^4$ rows by $10^4$ sweeps would see one; at the
S1 gate's limit, $2.3\times10^{-11}$, about one fit in 430.
\item[B$'$.] \emph{A, plus one more precision level.} The close calls are re-evaluated in double-double
arithmetic (about 32 digits). The floor falls below $10^{-24}$ at the points tested, so the count is zero in any
run anyone will make. Build effort: beyond A's real double-double functions, their complex versions and expm1,
sine and the complex square root, with error bounds, and the node formula at that precision: a few hundred lines
and their tests, about 1.3 to 1.5 times the work of A. Run time: none on average, since the second level runs
about once in $10^{11}$ steps at the points tested.
\item[B.] \emph{A full precision ladder.} Close calls are refined to any precision, so the decision is exactly the
ideal decision. Build effort: a multiple-precision library with rigorous functions (MPFR and MPC, or Arb), a new
system dependency on every platform CRAN builds for, plus the code. Run time: none on average. What it buys over
B$'$: nothing a user can observe, and it does not make the step exact in the implemented arithmetic, since the
proposal is still drawn with floating-point gamma generators and 32-bit uniforms (Section~\ref{sec:ideal}).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Recommendation: A, with the lead term in double-double and the floor measured in S1.} It holds the step
to the standard every other sampler in the package meets, and at the points tested its floor is far below what
the step's own random numbers already carry. B$'$ if the design's gate ``the tally is
zero in every gate cell'' should hold with certainty rather than with overwhelming probability; B is not
recommended.

\subsection*{Question 2: what should setData and setState do at fractional shapes?}

\textbf{Background.} When the code that runs the BART sampler inside a larger sampler changes the data between
sweeps, the rows whose latent distribution changed are marked and redrawn from scratch at the next sweep. At a
fractional shape (real frequency weights, dec-B568) no from-scratch draw exists, so the design refused the other
setters at such a change. setData and setState are not refused; the design moves their marked fractional rows by the step from
the value the row had. That is exact if the larger sampler reads the latents and conditions on them. It is not
exact if the larger sampler integrates the latents out and calls setData between sweeps with data that change
such a row: the step then starts from
a value drawn under the old data and does not forget it in one sweep, a bias of the kind dec-B589 removed for the
ordinal cutpoints (about 1.5\% in total variation in the exact toy of Theorem~\ref{thm:setters}). setState is
the same question when the state it installs was saved under other data than the sampler now holds; a state saved
under the data now installed changes no row's distribution, and the step from it is exact. Whatever the answer,
real weights are costlier than the design assumed: at fractional shapes the step is the only exact draw, and with
the error bound as specified a row step costs about 40 to 90 microseconds at $|\psi|$ up to 50 and about 320 at
150, against the design's 7 (Section~\ref{sec:changes}, item 8).

\textbf{Options.}
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] \emph{Refuse setData and setState at fractional shapes once a chain has run}, as the other setters are
refused; both stay allowed before the first sweep (a start, including restoring a saved state into a new
sampler). Cost to a user: a caller with real weights who wants to swap data or restore a state mid-chain cannot;
they build a new sampler and burn in again, or use whole-number weights. Build: one check and a message.
\item[(ii)] \emph{Allow it, documented as a restart.} Cost to a user: after each call the chain must burn in
again. For a caller that calls setData every sweep, as dec-B589 contemplates, every sweep is a restart and no
draw is from the posterior; the bias is unknown and silent. Build: documentation.
\item[(iii)] \emph{Allow it, documented as exact only for a caller that reads the latents.} Cost to a user: a caller
that integrates them out gets a silent bias, and the engine cannot tell which kind of caller it serves. Build:
documentation.
\item[(iv)] \emph{Mark only what changed.} setData compares each row's response, weight, offset and predictors
with the installed ones, marks only the rows that differ, and refuses only if a marked row has a fractional shape;
setState, which cannot tell under which data its state was saved, stays refused mid-chain at fractional shapes.
Cost to a user: none beyond (i), and a caller that hands the same data every sweep, or changes only whole-number
rows, is served exactly. Build: a row-by-row comparison at each call (one pass over the data, a fraction of a
sweep) and its tests, about twice (i).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Recommendation: (i) now, (iv) if a caller needs it.} (i) keeps every caller exact at no run-time cost and
matches the other setters; (iv) is (i) refined so that only an impossible request is refused. (ii) and (iii) leave
a silent bias.

\textbf{Ruling (dec-B607, 2026-11-06).} Option (iii), extended to every setter. The maintainer: ``I like option
(iii) because it empowers a user. [...] What's the harm in trusting the model writing and maybe including a short
example in the docs?''; shown that (iii) by the same reasoning lifts the design's refusals at the other setters:
``Yes, as long as there is a way of doing it correctly with the tools we have (or are cheap to build) and we can
document it, that seems enough for me.'' The rule: at fractional shapes every between-sweep setter, and setState,
is taken as at whole-number shapes, the rows it marks served by the step from their installed values; the help of
the setters and of getLatents says that changing data mid-chain is exact when the larger sampler updates its own
unknowns from their conditional given the latents, that when its update ignores them a changed row with a
fractional weight is left with a small bias, so such a sampler keeps whole-number weights on the rows it changes,
and that an update reading the latents while targeting the conditional without them is exact in neither case; the
documentation carries a short example, a logistic random-intercept sampler that reads getLatents, draws the
intercepts from their Gaussian conditional given the latents and the trees' fit, and installs them with
setOffset.

\section{Not proven}\label{sec:notproven}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A sharp certified $M^*$.} Appendix~\ref{app:explicit}'s bound is explicit but loose by many orders of
magnitude in the bulk at large $b$ or $|\psi|$. A certified sup of $f/q$ (grid values with certified densities plus
a certified bound between grid points, and the tail bounds of Appendix~\ref{app:explicit}) is not built. So the
rate $(1-1/M^*)^n$ and Theorem~\ref{thm:mitro}'s constant have no certified numerical value, and rejection
sampling from $q$ (which would give exact fresh draws at fractional shapes) is not available.
\item \textbf{The engine's library.} The numbers here are certified in ball arithmetic (Arb), whose functions carry
proven error bounds. A double-precision engine build relies on (A2) for its math library, which is assumed, not
proven for any particular library, and on double-double functions with error at most $2^{-100}$ for the lead and
the cache (A2$'$, Appendix~\ref{app:round}); the radii of a hand-written implementation with the rules of
Appendix~\ref{app:round} were not measured and may exceed Arb's 53-bit radii by a small factor (an S1 gate).
\item \textbf{Finiteness at one precision everywhere.} The enclosure is valid wherever it is finite, and with a
precision ladder the evaluation succeeds at every $x$; that 53 bits suffice at every reachable $x$ was checked at
the pins of Appendix~\ref{app:num}, not proven. The floor figures are measured at those points, in ball
arithmetic with the lead at 106 or 100 bits, not bounded over the whole range or measured in an engine.
\item \textbf{Cost.} The certified evaluator's cost is counted in the scripts and priced at kernel times, not timed
in an engine; the cheaper certificate of Section~\ref{sec:cheap} is proven but not built, so whether it brings the
cost near the main sum's is open (Section~\ref{sec:changes}, item 8).
\item \textbf{The floors of today's samplers} (Section~\ref{sec:ideal}) are bounded per comparison; the number of
comparisons per draw and the gamma generator's own approximation error were not measured.
\item \textbf{Convergence from a fixed start} of the whole sampler (Harris recurrence, or $\varphi$-irreducibility
and aperiodicity of today's sweep) is assumed, as for today's sampler.
\item \textbf{Efficiency.} Nothing here bounds acceptance rates or effective draws beyond Theorem~\ref{thm:row}'s
rejection bound $2\TV(f,q)$.
\item \textbf{The vertical-line rule} at small $b$ was not run.
\item \textbf{Multinomial:} only that the step with persistent latents and the existing forest step is not exact;
not that no exact persistent-latent scheme exists.
\item \textbf{Ranges.} The proofs hold for all $b>0$ and real $\psi$; numerical checks cover $b\in[0.2,10^5]$,
$|\psi|\le150$, with the proposal pinned up to \QPINMAXK{} terms. \QPINKNOTE
\item \textbf{Literature not read.} \cite{tierney}, \cite{mt} and \cite{wt} could not be fetched. Nothing here
depends on them: reversibility and the convergence theorems are cited from \cite{rr}, the converse of uniform
ergodicity for an unbounded weight is proven directly (Proposition~\ref{prop:K0}), and the contour lemmas are
proven here rather than taken from \cite{wt}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Rounding}\label{app:round}

\paragraph{What the scripts do.} Every certified number in this note is computed in ball arithmetic (Arb through
python-flint, \cite{arb}): a real or complex quantity is held as a midpoint $m$ and a radius $r\ge0$, the true value
lying in the ball $\{|v-m|\le r\}$, and each operation returns a ball that contains the image of its input balls,
its radius rounded upward. These are not first-order statements; each holds exactly. Arb's elementary functions
($\exp$, $\log$, $\mathrm{log1p}$, $\mathrm{expm1}$, $\sin$, $\cos$, $\sinh$, $\cosh$, real and complex square root
and logarithm) are rigorous in the same sense. The working precision $p$ (53 or 106 bits) sets the midpoints'
rounding, so radii are of order $2^{-p}$ times the size of the quantities combined, absolute and relative by
construction; the radius of $\log p(x)$ reported in Table~\ref{tab:floor} and in the pins is the full radius,
rounding and mathematical error together. Inputs are exact doubles ($x$, $\psi$, the sampler's $r_k,\alpha,\beta$),
so nothing is charged for representing them, and $\sigma=\psi^2/2$ is formed inside the arithmetic. This replaces
the first version's first-order analysis and its multiplicative inflation, which shrank negative logarithms and
did not scale with $b$; the first version's script is superseded by \texttt{arbpg.py}.

\paragraph{The rules, for a double-precision engine build.} An engine without a ball library can carry the same
enclosures in double precision with these rules, each exact under (A1)--(A2), with $u=2^{-53}$ and
$\gamma_n=nu/(1-nu)$ \cite[Lemma~3.1]{higham}. Every radius is computed in floating point with its own rounding
covered: either in upward rounding, or, in round to nearest, a radius formed by $n$ operations (a sum of $m$ terms
counting $m-1$) is multiplied by $1+\gamma_n$ and increased by $n$ times the smallest positive double, for
underflow. For the node sum and the truncation's dropped-node sums $n$ is the number of terms, up to $2\times10^4$
under the scripts' caps, so $\gamma_n\le2.3\times10^{-12}$, still a negligible relative change of a radius.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Sum.} $\widehat{x+y}=\fl(m_x+m_y)$, radius $r_x+r_y+u|\widehat{x+y}|$. A sum of $n$ terms computed
recursively has error at most $\gamma_{n-1}\sum|m_i|$ \cite[Sec.~4.2]{higham}, added to $\sum r_i$.
\item \emph{Product.} $|xy-m_xm_y|\le|m_x|r_y+|m_y|r_x+r_xr_y$, plus $u|\fl(m_xm_y)|$; for complex balls the same with
moduli, plus $\gamma_2\cdot\sqrt2$ times the product of moduli for the complex multiplication itself. Quotient:
$|x/y-m_x/m_y|\le(|m_x|r_y+|m_y|r_x)/(|m_y|(|m_y|-r_y))$ for $r_y<|m_y|$, plus $u|\fl(m_x/m_y)|$.
\item \emph{Exponential.} $|e^x-e^{m}|\le e^{m}\,\mathrm{expm1}(r)$ for real $x$ (complex: $|e^{m}|\mathrm{expm1}(r)$),
plus the library's $u_L|e^m|$; no premise on the size of $r$ (this replaces the first version's factor $1.01$,
which assumed $\mathrm{expm1}(r)<0.01$ without checking it).
\item \emph{Logarithm.} $|\log x-\log m|\le-\mathrm{log1p}(-r/|m|)$ for $r<|m|$, plus $u_L|\log m|$;
$\mathrm{log1p}$ likewise with $1+m$ in place of $m$.
\item \emph{Analytic functions} ($\sqrt{\cdot}$, $\mathrm{expm1}$, $\sin$, $\cos$, $\cosh$): $|g(x)-g(m)|\le r\sup|g'|$
over the ball, with the supremum bounded from the ball's ends (for $\sqrt W$ with $|W|>r$:
$r/(2\sqrt{|m|-r})$), plus the library's error.
\item \emph{Branch cuts.} For the principal complex square root, logarithm and log1p the two rules above hold only
on a ball that misses the branch cut (the closed negative real axis; for log1p, the real points at or below $-1$).
Where the true argument is known to lie in the closed upper half plane, as $W=W_0+\Delta$ at a node with $y\ge0$
($\mathrm{Im}\,\Delta=2\rho y$) and $1+2\Delta/(2\tau_j^2+t_0)$ for the finite family, the ball is first intersected
with that half plane: on it the principal branch, continued to the cut from above, is continuous, and the
derivative bound over the intersection (a convex set) applies. For the log1p of \eqref{eq:cf}, a ball of its
argument plus 1 that meets the cut is replaced by the direct form $\lambda(w)-\lambda(w_0)$, whose
$\mathrm{log1p}(e^{-2w})$ has $|e^{-2w}|<1$ and never meets it. Any other ball that meets a cut is widened to contain
both one-sided limits, or the evaluation is marked undecided. Arb does the equivalent internally.
\item \emph{Log-domain majorants.} A bound $\log M\le\hat\ell$ is valid when $\hat\ell$ is the computed value plus the
error bound of the terms that formed it (the sum rule on the terms of the logarithm), never the computed value
times a factor: a factor $1+\epsilon$ shrinks a negative logarithm, and a fixed offset does not scale with the
terms, which at $b=10^5$ reach $10^6$.
\end{itemize}
The evaluator built this way is the one \texttt{arbpg.py} specifies; its radii are larger than Arb's by the
library's $u_L$ terms and the slack of the bounds above, a small factor that was not measured.

\paragraph{The lead and the cache in double-double.} A double-double number is an unevaluated sum of two doubles.
The lead \eqref{eq:lead} and the update \eqref{eq:tiltupd} are carried with the same rules, $u$ replaced by
$u_2=16u^2=2^{-102}$ and $\gamma_n$ by $\gamma^{(2)}_n=nu_2/(1-nu_2)$. $u_2$ bounds the relative error of the
accurate double-double addition, multiplication and division of \cite{jmp}. The functions $\exp$, $\log$,
$\mathrm{log1p}$, $\sin$ and $\sqrt{\cdot}$ in double-double are assumed to have a stated relative error
$u_{L2}\le2^{-100}$ (A2$'$, the counterpart of (A2); the scripts' 100-bit stand-in is this case). The inputs $x$,
$t_0$, $b$, $\psi$, $a_j$, $\mu_j$ are exact doubles, and a sum or product of two doubles is exact in
double-double, so $A_\psi^2$, $\psi'-\psi$ and $\psi'+\psi$ carry no error. Counting operations as above: the
radius of $\ell_q$ is at most $(\gamma^{(2)}_{K+4}+u_{L2})(|xt_0|+\sum_ja_j|\mathrm{log1p}(t_0/\mu_j)|)$, and that
of $\ell_f$ at most $(\gamma^{(2)}_{12}+2u_{L2})(|xt_0|+b|\lambda(w_0)|+b|\lambda(A_\psi)|)+R_W$. Here $R_W$ carries
the rounding of $W_0$ (radius $u_2|W_0|$) through $W\mapsto\lambda(\sqrt W)$, whose derivative is
$\tanh(w_0)/(2w_0)\le\frac12$, or through $W\mapsto\log\cos\sqrt{-W}$, whose derivative $\tan(v)/(2v)$, $v=\sqrt{-W_0}$,
is unbounded as $v\to\pi/2$; so the build charges $R_W=b\,u_2|W_0|\,D$ explicitly, where $D$ is the derivative
at the lower end of $W_0$'s enclosure ($D$ decreases in $W$ across both branches, so this bounds it on the
enclosure): $D=\tanh(w)/(2w)$ there on $W_0\ge0$, $D=\tan(v)/(2v)$ on $W_0<0$. At the saddle $b\tan(v)/(4v)=x$ (the
saddle equation at $w_0=iv$), so there $R_W=2u_2x|W_0|\le u_2x|t_0|$, as $|W_0|\le|t_0|/2$ when $W_0<0$; on
$W_0\ge0$, $R_W$ is $b\,u_2w_0\tanh(w_0)/2\le b\,u_2w_0/2$ up to a factor $1+O(u_2)$, and at most $u_2b\lambda(w_0)$, as
$w\tanh w/2\le\log\cosh w$ (the difference is 0 at 0 and increasing); both are inside the count above,
but $t_0$ is solved only approximately, so the explicit charge is what the build uses. $\log\cos v$ is formed as
$\log\sin(\pi/2-v)$ with $\pi/2$ held in double-double, so that (A2$'$) needs a relative error bound for $\sin$ near
0 only; the absolute error this leaves, $b(\pi/2)u_2/(\pi/2-v)$, is at the saddle about $\pi^2xu_2\le2u_2x|t_0|$
($|t_0|\ge\pi^2/2$ when $v$ is near $\pi/2$), again inside the count. Over $b\le10^5$, $|\psi|\le150$ and $|z|\le20$ these sums are at most
about $1.5\times10^7$ for $f$ and $1.1\times10^5$ for $q$, so both radii are below $10^{-22}$. The update's radius is
small: in the difference form of Section~\ref{sec:floor} its terms are of size $b|\psi'-\psi|$ and $x|\psi'^2-\psi^2|/2$, at most about $10^6$ for
$|\psi'-\psi|\le5$, so the radius is below $10^{-23}$. Rounding the cached value to double is avoided by storing
it in double-double.

\paragraph{The left series.} Its terms are computed in multiple precision in \texttt{refs.py} (a reference only); the
certified left-series bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:left} is not used by the pins and needs, in an engine build,
the same rules for its recurrence and its sum.

\section{An explicit bound on the weight}\label{app:explicit}

Untilted form: $w=\kappa\,\fz/\qt$ with $\kappa=\cosh^b(\psi/2)\widetilde L(\sigma)$ by \eqref{eq:qtilt}. Let
$\tilde\rho_k=r_k-\sigma$ ($k\le K$, so $\tilde\rho_1\le c_1$), $\tilde\beta=\beta-\sigma$ (the largest rate of
$\qt$), $A=Kb+\alpha$, $J=\lceil1/b\rceil$, $\tilde c=\prod_{k\le K}(\tilde\rho_k/\tilde\beta)^b$,
$\Pi_J=\prod_{k=2}^J(c_k/c_1)^b$, $E_J=\prod_{k>J}(1-c_1/c_k)^{-b}$ (with $\prod_{k\ge2}(1-c_1/c_k)=\pi/4$),
$Y_q=\sum_{k=2}^K\Ga(b,\tilde\rho_k)+\Ga(\alpha,\tilde\beta)$, $\tilde\mu=\sum_{k=2}^Kb/(\tilde\rho_k-\tilde\rho_1)+
\alpha/(\tilde\beta-\tilde\rho_1)$, $E_q=\E e^{\tilde\rho_1Y_q}$, and $x_s=(b+1)/(2\log(2C_b))$. Choose $\theta\in
(0,1]$ ($\theta<1$ if $b>1$) and $X>\tilde\mu/\theta$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:explicit}
$M^*\le\max(M_A,M_B,M_C)$, where
\begin{align*}
M_A&=\frac{\kappa\,2^{b-1}b\,\Gamma(A)}{\tilde c\sqrt{2\pi}\,\tilde\beta^A}\,e^{\tilde\beta x_s}\,
\Bigl(\frac{8(A+\frac12)}{b^2}\Bigr)^{A+\frac12}e^{-(A+\frac12)}\quad(x\le x_s),\\
M_B&=\frac{\kappa\,\Pi_JE_J}{\tilde c}\max_{x\in\{x_s,X\}}\frac{g_{Jb,c_1}(x)}{g_{A,\tilde\beta}(x)}\quad
(x_s\le x\le X;\ \text{omit if }x_s\ge X),\\
M_C&=\frac{\kappa\,(c_1/\tilde\rho_1)^bB}{E_q(1-\theta)^{(b-1)_+}(1-\tilde\mu/(\theta X))}\quad(x\ge X),
\end{align*}
with $B=E_1=(\pi/4)^{-b}$ if $b\ge1$, and for $b<1$ $B=2^{1-b}(\pi/4)^{-b}+\frac{\Pi'E'}{b2^b}
\sup_{y\ge X/2}2y\,e^{c_1y}g_{Jb,c_2}(y)$, $\Pi'=\prod_{k=3}^{J+1}(c_k/c_2)^b$,
$E'=\prod_{k\ge J+2}(1-c_2/c_k)^{-b}$, the supremum attained at $y=\max(X/2,Jb/(c_2-c_1))$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$x\le x_s$: $\fz\le a_0$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:left}), $\qt\ge\tilde cg_{A,\tilde\beta}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:merge});
\[a_0/g_{A,\tilde\beta}=\frac{2^{b-1}b\,\Gamma(A)}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\tilde\beta^A}\,x^{-A-1/2}e^{-b^2/(8x)+\tilde\beta x},\]
and
$x^{-A-1/2}e^{-b^2/(8x)}$ is maximal at $x=b^2/(8(A+\frac12))$. $x_s\le x\le X$: merge the first $J$ terms of
$\fz$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:merge}) into $\Pi_Jg_{Jb,c_1}$ and apply Lemma~\ref{lem:gconv}(i) with $a=Jb\ge1$; the ratio
$g_{Jb,c_1}/g_{A,\tilde\beta}\propto x^{Jb-A}e^{(\tilde\beta-c_1)x}$ has a logarithm that is convex (if $A\ge Jb$)
or increasing (if $A<Jb$), so its maximum on an interval is at an end. $x\ge X$: $\fz=g_{b,c_1}\Phi_f$ with
$\Phi_f\le B$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:gconv}(i) or (ii) (for (ii), merge the terms $k=2,\dots,J+1$ of $Y_f$ and use (i)
for the density bound $p_{Y_f}\le\Pi'E'g_{Jb,c_2}$, and $x\le2y$ on $y\ge x/2$); $\qt=g_{b,\tilde\rho_1}\Phi_q$ with
$\Phi_q\ge E_q(1-\theta)^{(b-1)_+}(1-\tilde\mu/(\theta x))$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:gconv}(iii), increasing in $x$; and
$g_{b,c_1}/g_{b,\tilde\rho_1}\le(c_1/\tilde\rho_1)^b$.
\end{proof}
Every factor is finite, so this proves Proposition~\ref{prop:bounded} with constants. It is useless for rates
(script \texttt{explicit\_m.py}, 40 digits, $\theta$ and $X$ optimized on a grid): about $10^{18}$ at
$(b,\psi,K)=(1,0,1)$, $10^{582}$ at $(16,8,3)$ and $10^{1000}$ at $(0.2,150,1)$, against certified lower bounds of
1.07, 1.005 and 35.6; $M_A$ dominates every case, $M_C$ is between $10^2$ and $10^5$. in
$M_A$ and $M_B$ the factor $e^{\tilde\beta x}$ with $\tilde\beta\ge c_{K+1}$ is astronomically large wherever the
$K$ rule takes many terms, and $M_C$'s $(1-\theta)^{-(b-1)}$ grows exponentially in $b$.

What the pins cover: they test that the enclosure contains the true value at the points tested. The enclosure is
an a priori bound computed at run time, so the pins check the implementation, not the theorem, and a point not
pinned is covered by the same bound; what is not covered is whether 53 bits suffice to make the enclosure finite
and narrow everywhere (Section~\ref{sec:notproven}).
