\section*{Summary for the maintainer}

\textbf{What the step is.} Logistic, binomial and negative binomial fits carry one Polya-Gamma latent per row.
Today each latent is redrawn from scratch every sweep by a sum whose cost grows with the row's shape. The step
studied here proposes a new latent from a fast approximation (the first $K$ terms of the latent's exact series
plus one gamma matched to the rest) and keeps the proposal or the old value with the Metropolis-Hastings
probability, computed from the exact density of the latent and of the proposal. The densities have no closed
form; they are computed numerically with a proven error bound, and the keep-or-move decision is taken only when
that bound cannot change it.

\textbf{What is proven.} Under exact arithmetic and exact random numbers (the idealization every sampler in the
package is proven under, Section~\ref{sec:ideal}): the step leaves each row's latent distribution exactly
invariant for every shape $b>0$, fit value and $K\ge1$, and converges from any value (Theorem~\ref{thm:row}); a
decision taken under the bound is the exact decision, and refining the numerical rule always resolves it
(Section~\ref{sec:dec}); inside the sweep every family the design gives the step keeps the joint posterior,
including the negative binomial with an estimated dispersion and the per-row choice between the step and the sum
(Sections~\ref{sec:sweep}--\ref{sec:rule}); rows a setter changed are exact when drawn fresh, for the two ways of
using the latents defined in Section~\ref{sec:setters}; a starting value needs no exact draw.

\textbf{Floating point.} No sampler in the package is exact in floating point: each of its comparisons against a
32-bit uniform is off by up to $2^{-32}$, so today's exact sum carries up to about $10^{-9}$ per unit of shape per draw.
The first version of this note evaluated the densities in a form whose rounding grew with the size of the
log-density (to $1.4\times10^{-8}$ at the largest shapes), so that up to one step in ten million was too close to
call. The density is now evaluated in a form whose rounding does not grow that way. Over shapes 0.2 to $10^5$ and
$|\psi|$ to 150, at every point tested, each log-density is certified in interval arithmetic to within \FLOORDENS{}
and contains independent references, provided one number per point, the lead term, is carried in double-double
arithmetic (about 32 digits), as the engine must do it; in plain double that term alone reaches $10^{-10}$ far from
the mean. At those points a step is too close to call \FLOORSTEPWORDS{} (Section~\ref{sec:floor}). That is below the
$2^{-32}$ that each of the step's own random comparisons already carries. The engine's own figure is to be measured
at the same points when it is built (Section~\ref{sec:changes}).

\textbf{Cost.} The step evaluates three densities a row, as the design priced, when the row's previous step left
its density in the cache (the current point's density is carried from the previous sweep by an exact update), and
four otherwise. But a certified density costs far more than the design's uncertified sizing, and that decides
where the feature pays. With the error bound as the scripts compute it, the step is slower than today's exact sum
at every shape tested when $|\psi|=150$, and pays only from shapes of a few hundred at $|\psi|\le8$ and of several
thousand at $|\psi|=50$; at fractional shapes, where there is no sum, a step costs 40 to 320 microseconds against
the design's 7. A cheaper error bound, proven here but not built, could make the step pay from about a thousand
trials at every $|\psi|$ tested. So S1 must build and time it, and the choice between the sum and the step is then
set from S1's timings by $|\psi|$ as well as by shape (Section~\ref{sec:changes}, item 8).

\textbf{Where it is not exact.} (1) Replacing an undecided decision by a fresh exact draw. (2) The step at a row
a setter changed, when the larger sampler integrates the latents out; at a fractional shape the design reaches
this through every between-sweep setter and setState, all of which it takes as ruled (Section~\ref{sec:maint},
Question~2), its help telling such a sampler to keep whole-number weights on the rows it changes
(Section~\ref{sec:setters}). (3) Multinomial fits.
(4) Choosing between the step and the sum by looking at the latent's own value. (5) A larger sampler whose step
fits neither of the two ways defined in Section~\ref{sec:setters}. (6) An activity change at a fractional
shape, except on the pseudo-prior extension of Section~\ref{sec:setters}.

\textbf{What is not proven.} A sharp bound on how far the proposal can fall below the target, so the speed of
convergence has no usable number. The math library's accuracy, for the engine's double-precision version (the
scripts here use interval arithmetic and need no such assumption). The floor over every point rather than the
points tested, and in the engine's arithmetic rather than the scripts'. The cost of a built evaluator. Convergence
of the whole BART sampler from a fixed start, assumed as today.

\textbf{Two questions for you} (Section~\ref{sec:maint}): what ``exact'' should mean once arithmetic is floating
point (recommended: the package's existing standard, with the new density form and its lead term in
double-double); and what setData and setState should do at fractional shapes (recommended: refuse once a chain has
run, as the other setters were to be refused; ruled otherwise on 2026-11-06: every setter and setState is taken at
fractional shapes, and the help says when that is exact). \textbf{What the design must change} is in Section~\ref{sec:changes}: the
density form and its lead term, the cache, the cost figures, the $K$ table at $|\psi|=150$ (140 to 560 terms, not
110 to 120), the negative binomial $\psi$, the floor figure and its measurement, and the setters.

\section{Setting, notation and assumptions}\label{sec:setting}

\paragraph{The latent distribution.} For $b>0$ and real $\psi$ let $c_k=2\pi^2(k-\tfrac12)^2$,
$\sigma=\psi^2/2$, $d_k=c_k+\sigma$ ($k\ge1$). $\PG(b,\psi)$ is the distribution of $\sum_{k\ge1}G_k$ with
independent $G_k\sim\Ga(b,d_k)$ (shape $b$, rate $d_k$) \cite[eq.~(1) and Thm.~1]{psw}. Its Laplace transform is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lt}
  L_{b,\psi}(t)=\E e^{-tX}=\prod_{k\ge1}\Bigl(1+\frac t{d_k}\Bigr)^{-b}
  =\frac{\cosh^b(\psi/2)}{\cosh^b\sqrt{(\sigma+t)/2}},\qquad t>-d_1,
\end{equation}
by the product $\cosh u=\prod_k(1+u^2/(\pi^2(k-\frac12)^2))$. Its density is the exponential tilt
$f_{b,\psi}(x)=\cosh^b(\psi/2)\,e^{-\sigma x}f_{b,0}(x)$ \cite[Sec.~2.3 and Thm.~1]{psw}; write $f=f_{b,\psi}$, $\fz=f_{b,0}$.
In the model, rows are conditionally independent given everything else, row $i$'s latent $\omega_i$ having
distribution $\PG(b_i,\psi_i)$ with $b_i$ the row's shape and $\psi_i$ its log-odds: for logistic and binomial
the linear predictor $\eta_i$ (fit plus offset), for the negative binomial with dispersion $r$, $\psi_i=\eta_i-\log
r$, since the success probability is $\mu_i/(\mu_i+r)$ (the engine's \texttt{NBResponse::anchor} is offset plus
centering constant minus $\log r$) \cite[Sec.~3 and Thm.~1]{psw}.

\paragraph{The proposal.} Fix $K\ge1$. Let $m=\sum_{k>K}d_k^{-1}$, $v=\sum_{k>K}d_k^{-2}$, $\alpha=bm^2/v$,
$\beta=m/v$, so that $\Ga(\alpha,\beta)$ has the mean $bm$ and variance $bv$ of $\sum_{k>K}G_k$. The proposal
$q=q_{b,\psi,K}$ is the distribution of $\sum_{k\le K}\Ga(b,r_k)+\Ga(\alpha,\beta)$, where $r_k$ are the rates the
sampler actually uses (floating-point values of $d_k$; Section~\ref{sec:changes} requires $r_1\le d_1$). The
values $\alpha,\beta$ used are likewise the sampler's doubles; any positive values give a valid proposal, so
rounding in them changes efficiency only. Since $\beta\ge d_{K+1}>\sigma$ (each $d_k^{-1}\ge d_{K+1}d_k^{-2}$
for $k>K$), every rate exceeds $\sigma$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qtilt}
  q(x)=e^{-\sigma x}\qt(x)/\widetilde L(\sigma),\qquad \widetilde L(\sigma)
  =\prod_{k\le K}\Bigl(\frac{r_k-\sigma}{r_k}\Bigr)^b\Bigl(\frac{\beta-\sigma}{\beta}\Bigr)^\alpha,
\end{equation}
with $\qt$ the density of $\sum_{k\le K}\Ga(b,r_k-\sigma)+\Ga(\alpha,\beta-\sigma)$, the \emph{untilted
proposal}. The \emph{weight} is $w=f/q$ and $M^*=\sup_{x>0}w(x)$.

\paragraph{The step.} The independence Metropolis-Hastings (IMH) kernel with target $f$ and proposal $q$:
from $x$, draw $y\sim q$ and $U\sim\mathrm{Unif}(0,1)$; move to $y$ if $U<a(x,y)$, else stay, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio}
  a(x,y)=\min\{1,\,r(x,y)\},\qquad
  r(x,y)=\frac{f(y)q(x)}{f(x)q(y)}=\frac{\fz(y)\,\qt(x)}{\fz(x)\,\qt(y)} .
\end{equation}
The second (untilted) form follows from \eqref{eq:qtilt} and the tilt. The two are equal in exact arithmetic;
the step evaluates the first, from $\log f$ and $\log q$ directly. The untilted form, which the first version of
this note used, evaluates $\fz$ far in its left tail, where $|\log\fz|$ reaches $4\times10^6$ and rounding grows with
it (Section~\ref{sec:floor}). In the tilted form $f$ is evaluated at the engine's $\psi$ and $q$ at the
sampler's own doubles $r_k,\alpha,\beta$, so nothing has to cancel between them: the rounding of
$\sigma=\psi^2/2$ never enters, and $q$ is exactly the density of the proposal drawn (under (A0)).

The ratio needs four log-densities. $\log q(x)$ is evaluated afresh each sweep, as the design priced: the
proposal is re-matched at the current $\psi$ and is not a tilt. $\log f(x)$ need not be. The design cached
$\log\fz(x)$, which does not depend on $\psi$; in the tilted form the cache holds $\log f_{b,\psi}(x)$ from the
previous sweep and moves it to the new $\psi'$ by the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tiltupd}
  \log f_{b,\psi'}(x)=\log f_{b,\psi}(x)+b\,\bigl[\lambda(|\psi'|/2)-\lambda(|\psi|/2)\bigr]
  -\frac x2\,(\psi'-\psi)(\psi'+\psi),
\end{equation}
with $\lambda(a)=a-\log2+\mathrm{log1p}(e^{-2a})=\log\cosh a$ for $a\ge0$: the tilt formula at $\psi'$ divided by
that at $\psi$. Its two terms are
of size $b|\psi'-\psi|$, not $b\log\cosh(\psi/2)$, and are computed in double-double (Section~\ref{sec:floor},
``The cache''). The entry is valid only for the point it was computed at: it stores that point as the exact double
$x$, with $b$ and $\psi$, and is used only when $x$ equals the row's current $\omega_i$ bit for bit and $b$ is
unchanged. setData, setState, a fresh draw by the sum and an outer step that writes the latents can each replace
$\omega_i$ with $b$ and $\psi$ unchanged (setData with only the response changed leaves $\PG(b_i,\psi_i)$ as it
was), and a stale entry would then give a wrong $\log r$ silently; the match on $x$ excludes that. So a step makes
three density evaluations, $f(y)$, $q(y)$ and $q(x)$, and one update (two double-double exponentials and
log1p's and about ten double-double operations, a few hundred double operations), the count the design priced, whenever the row's previous step
was a RowStep that left a valid entry. Otherwise, after a fresh draw by the sum (a marked row, the first sweep, a
row the rule moved from the sum, every negative binomial row after $r$ moved) or any other write to $\omega_i$, it
makes four. The design's cache of $\log\fz(\omega_i)$ had the same gap. In the design's estimated-$r$ negative
binomial cell, where $r$ moved on 38 percent of sweeps, about 38 percent of steps make four evaluations.
Rebuilding $\log f(x)$ from a cached $\log\fz(x)$ would not do: that value carries the untilted form's large
rounding.

\paragraph{Families evaluated.} Both $f$ and $q$ are densities of $X=\sum_j\Ga(a_j,2\tau_j^2)$ with
$0<\tau_1\le\tau_2\le\cdots$ (finitely or infinitely many terms) and Laplace transform
$L(t)=\prod_j(1+t/(2\tau_j^2))^{-a_j}$: for $f$, $2\tau_k^2=d_k$, $a_k=b$ and
$L(t)=\cosh^b(A_\psi)/\cosh^b\sqrt{A_\psi^2+t/2}$, $A_\psi=|\psi|/2$; for $q$, $K+1$ terms with $2\tau_k^2=r_k$ and
$2\tau_{K+1}^2=\beta$. Total shape $A=\sum a_j$ ($A=\infty$ for $f$). The case $\psi=0$ is $\fz$; with rates
$r_k-\sigma$, $\beta-\sigma$ the finite family is $\qt$.

\paragraph{Assumptions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item[(A0)] The idealization of Section~\ref{sec:ideal}: uniforms are exact real uniforms, gamma variates exact
draws, the state ($\omega_i$, the proposal $y$, the trees) real-valued, sums such as the proposal's
$\sum_kG_k$ exact, and the model's quantities ($b_i$, $\psi_i$, the fits) the values the engine computes. Every
exactness theorem below holds under (A0). What floating point changes is in Section~\ref{sec:ideal}.
\item[(A1)] IEEE 754 binary64 arithmetic: $+,-,\times,/$ and real square root correctly rounded, unit roundoff
$u=2^{-53}$. Used only for the engine's double-precision evaluation (Appendix~\ref{app:round}).
\item[(A2)] The engine's math library returns $\exp,\log,\mathrm{log1p},\mathrm{expm1},\sinh,\cosh,\tanh,\arctan$,
$\sin,\cos$ and the complex square root with a stated bound $u_L$ on the error (relative; absolute for $\sin,\cos$),
for example $u_L=2u$. This is an assumption about the library, not a theorem. The scripts that certify the numbers
in this note use ball arithmetic (Arb, \cite{arb}), whose functions carry rigorous error bounds, and need no
(A2).
\end{itemize}
Total variation is $\TV(\mu,\nu)=\sup_A|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|$. ``Exact'' means, as ruled (dec-B583, dec-B591): the
chain's long-run distribution is the posterior; operationally, the posterior is invariant and the chain converges
to it. Every theorem is stated under the idealization it needs; what exactness should mean in floating point is
put to the maintainer in Section~\ref{sec:maint}, not settled here.

\section{The algorithm}\label{sec:alg}

The latent step runs at the start of each sweep, after the per-sweep hook and before the trees. $\mathcal M$ is
the set of rows marked by setters since their last fresh draw. $\mathrm{Sum}(b,\psi)$ is today's exact draw (a sum
of $b$ exact $\PG(1,\psi)$ draws), available only for whole-number $b$.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Latent step at the start of a sweep}\label{alg:sweep}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{if} the family is negative binomial with estimated $r$: draw $r'$ from its distribution given $y$,
fits and offset with $\omega$ integrated out; $\mathit{moved}\gets(r'\neq r)$; $r\gets r'$
\For{each active row $i$} \Comment{rows are conditionally independent; any order}
  \State $b_i\gets$ the row's shape (nbinom: $y_i+r$); $\psi_i\gets$ its log-odds (nbinom: $\eta_i-\log r$)
  \If{($i\in\mathcal M$ \textbf{or} $\mathit{moved}$) \textbf{and} $b_i\in\mathbb Z$}
     \State $\omega_i\gets\mathrm{Sum}(b_i,\psi_i)$; clear $i$'s mark
  \ElsIf{$i\in\mathcal M$} \Comment{fractional $b_i$: marked by a setter or setState}
     \State $\omega_i\gets\textsc{RowStep}(\omega_i,b_i,\psi_i)$; clear $i$'s mark
     \Comment{not exact under contract I if the call changed the row (Thm.~\ref{thm:setters}(c))}
  \ElsIf{$b_i>64$ \textbf{or} $b_i\notin\mathbb Z$} \Comment{dec-B590}
     \State $\omega_i\gets\textsc{RowStep}(\omega_i,b_i,\psi_i)$
  \Else
     \State $\omega_i\gets\mathrm{Sum}(b_i,\psi_i)$
  \EndIf
  \State poll the run's cancel here, between rows only
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
No setter is refused at a fractional shape (the ruling on Section~\ref{sec:maint}'s second question): a marked
fractional row, after any between-sweep setter or setState, is served by the step from its installed value; line 6
records that, exact for a contract-C caller and not for a contract-I caller whose call changed the row.
A cancel polled inside a row's refinement would leave that row unchanged after a time that depends on
$\omega_i$, a choice read from $\omega_i$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:rule}); it is harmless only because a cancel ends the
run and a later run starts from that state as from any start (Theorem~\ref{thm:starts}), but polling between rows
avoids the question.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\textsc{RowStep}$(x,b,\psi)$: one IMH step with a certified decision}\label{alg:row}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State $K\gets$ the $K$ rule (any $K\ge1$ is exact; Section~\ref{sec:row}); form $r_k$ ($r_1\le d_1$), $\alpha$,
$\beta$ as doubles
\State $y\gets\sum_{k\le K}\Ga(b,r_k)+\Ga(\alpha,\beta)$ \Comment{exact draws under (A0)}
\State $U\gets$ a uniform, held as the interval $[U_-,U_+)$ its drawn bits determine
\State $j\gets0$; $I_{f(x)}\gets$ the cached enclosure of $\log f(x)$ moved to the current $\psi$ by
\eqref{eq:tiltupd} if the entry's point equals $x$ bit for bit and its $b$ is the row's; else $\R$; $I_\bullet\gets\R$ for $\log f(y),\log q(x),\log q(y)$
\Loop
  \State refine: at level $j$, evaluate $f(y)$, $q(x)$, $q(y)$ (Algorithm~\ref{alg:dens}), and $f(x)$ only if
  $I_{f(x)}$ is wider than level $j$ gives; intersect
  \State $[\ell_-,\ell_+]\gets$ the enclosure of $\log r(x,y)=\log f(y)+\log q(x)-\log f(x)-\log q(y)$ from the four
  \State \textbf{if} $\ell_-\ge\min(0,\log U_+)$: cache $(y,b,\psi,I_{f(y)})$; \Return $y$ \Comment{$U<r$ certain}
  \State \textbf{if} $\ell_+\le\log U_-$: cache $(x,b,\psi,I_{f(x)})$; \Return $x$ \Comment{$U\ge r$ certain}
  \State $j\gets j+1$; draw more bits of $U$ if its interval meets $[e^{\ell_-},e^{\ell_+}]$
\EndLoop
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The refinement levels are: smaller steps $h$ at a fixed precision, then, if the build has them, higher precisions
(a precision ladder, Section~\ref{sec:ladder}). A build with a fixed top precision replaces the loop, once that
level is exhausted, by a fixed decision (keep $x$) and a tally (Proposition~\ref{prop:floor}). The cache holds,
per row, $\log f$ at a point with its enclosure and level, the value in double-double, the point as an exact
double and the $(b,\psi)$ it was computed at; it is used only when the point equals the row's $\omega_i$ and $b$ is
unchanged, and then moved to the new $\psi$ by \eqref{eq:tiltupd}. $\log q(x)$ is not carried
across sweeps: the proposal's rates and remainder move with $\psi$, and $q$ is no tilt.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Log density with an enclosure, family $(a_j,\tau_j)$, point $x>0$}\label{alg:dens}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State solve $x=\sum_ja_j/(2\tau_j^2+t_0)$ for the saddle $t_0>-2\tau_1^2$ (for $f$: $x=b\tanh(w_0)/(4w_0)$,
$w_0^2=A_\psi^2+t_0/2$, the $\tan$ form if $w_0^2<0$); any $t_0$ is valid, double precision suffices
\State $T_1\gets\tau_1^2+t_0/2$; $\rho^2\gets\max\{T_1-\pi^2/4,\ T_1/2\}$ \Comment{any $\rho>0$ with $\rho^2<T_1$ is
valid}
\For{$h$ in $1,\tfrac34,\tfrac12,\tfrac38,\dots$ (so $kh$ is exact)}
   \State $\hat S\gets h[G(0)+2\sum_{k=1}^N\mathrm{Re}\,G(kh)]$, $G(y)=\exp\bigl(2x\Delta+\ell(\Delta)\bigr)(\rho+iy)$,
   $\Delta=-y^2+2i\rho y$, $\ell$ by \eqref{eq:cf}
   \State bound the discretization and truncation, $E$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:dens}); rounding is carried in the ball
   arithmetic
   \State \textbf{if} $\hat S>E$: $\log\hat p\gets\log\frac2\pi+xt_0+\log L(t_0)+\log(\hat S\pm E)$, the lead
   $xt_0+\log L(t_0)$ in its cancellation-free form (Section~\ref{sec:floor})
   \State stop when $E$ is below the rounding radius or stops improving
\EndFor
\State optional, at small $x$ for $\fz$-type families: the left series (Proposition~\ref{prop:left})
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Main results}\label{sec:main}

\begin{mainthm}
Under (A0), for every $b>0$, real $\psi$ and $K\ge1$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{T\arabic*.},leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \emph{Row kernel.} The IMH kernel $P$ with ratio \eqref{eq:ratio} is reversible with respect to
$\PG(b,\psi)$; $q>0$ on $(0,\infty)$; with the first rate not above $d_1$, $M^*=\sup f/q<\infty$, with
$\lim_{x\to\infty}w=e^{bD}$ at exact rates, $D>0$ in closed form, $\lim_{x\to0}w=0$ and an explicit (loose) bound on $M^*$; and
$\TV(P^n(x,\cdot),\PG(b,\psi))\le(1-1/M^*)^n$ for every $x$. The $K$ rule $bD\le0.01$ bounds the tail limit only:
$M^*>e^{bD}$ is certified at small $b$. With $K=0$, or with $r_1>d_1$, $M^*=\infty$ and the step is not
uniformly ergodic (it is still invariant and converges from almost every start).
\item \emph{Decision.} With valid enclosures (Arb, or (A1)--(A2) and Appendix~\ref{app:round}), a decision taken
when the enclosure of $\log r$ clears $\log U$ equals the exact decision. The undecided probability at a level of
half-width $E$ with $\beta$ bits of $U$ is at most $2E+2^{1-\beta}$; with a precision ladder whose bounds tend to
zero the step terminates with probability one and equals the exact kernel. Stopped at a top level with
$\bar E=2E_f+2^{1-\beta}$: the kernel is within $\bar E$ of the exact one in total variation per row, $N$ sweeps of
$n$ rows within $nN\bar E$, and the row chain's stationary distribution within $(\lceil M^*\log2\rceil+M^*)\bar E$.
A fixed top level is not exact. Replacing an undecided decision by a fresh exact draw does not keep $\PG(b,\psi)$
in general.
\item \emph{Densities.} For the contour and step of Algorithm~\ref{alg:dens}, $|\hat S-S|\le E$ plus the rounding,
with $E$ an a priori bound (discretization by two edge lines, truncation). In the tilted form, with the lead
$xt_0+\log L(t_0)$ in double-double and the rest at 53 bits, the radius of each log-density's enclosure in ball
arithmetic is at most \FLOORDENS{} at every pin over $b\in[0.2,10^5]$, $|\psi|\le150$ and $x$ from 4 standard
deviations below the mean to 12 above (against $1.4\times10^{-8}$ at the mean in the untilted form), and \FLOORHI{}
at 106 bits; the undecided probability per row step at the pins is then at most \FLOORSTEP. With the lead in plain
double the radius reaches $10^{-10}$ away from the mean. Every enclosure contained independent references at
every pin. The left series bound and the vertical-line rule hold as stated in
Section~\ref{sec:dens}.
\item \emph{Sweep.} With the latent step first and every other step invariant for the joint posterior, the sweep
is invariant. Only the trees (and, where present, the combiner's glue) read $\omega$ among the steps after it;
the level shift and the probit rescaling do not change any row's conditional.
\item \emph{Estimated nbinom $r$.} The collapsed $r$ step, then fresh exact $\omega$ where $r$ moved and the step
where it stayed, is invariant, for any $r$ kernel that is invariant for $r$'s marginal and does not read
$\omega$.
\item \emph{Per-row rule.} Any per-row choice between the sum and the step that depends on anything but the row's
own $\omega_i$ keeps the row's conditional; a choice read from $\omega_i$ can break it.
\item \emph{Setters.} Under contract C (the larger sampler's step conditions on $\omega$) the composite chain is
exact with fresh draws or the step at marked rows. Under contract I (it integrates $\omega$ out and does not read
it) it is exact with fresh draws at marked rows and not with the step at a row whose conditional changed; so the
design's step at fractional rows after any setter or setState that changes their $(b_i,\psi_i)$ or activity is not
exact under contract I (a call that changes no row's conditional leaves it exact). A remedy exact for every contract-I step either is a
fresh draw or uses the row's previous conditional. Outer steps of neither kind are not covered. Contract C's
result holds for a fixed set of rows; an activity change at a fractional shape is covered only on the pseudo-prior
extension.
\item \emph{Starts.} Exactness needs invariance and convergence from the start used; an exact draw at the start
is neither needed nor, with the trees at a fixed start, sufficient. The step inherits irreducibility and
aperiodicity from today's sampler.
\item \emph{Multinomial.} With persistent latents moved by the step, the forest steps are not invariant for the
extended target the step needs, and the scheme is not exact (an exact counterexample). Fresh draws are.
\end{enumerate}
In floating point every statement above holds for the idealized chain; the implemented chain differs from it by
the floors of Section~\ref{sec:ideal}, which every sampler in the package shares.
\end{mainthm}
